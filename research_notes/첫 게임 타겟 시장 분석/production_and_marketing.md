## 1. Engine choice in 2025-2026 (Unity, Godot, Unreal, GameMaker): pricing, share, learning curve, 2D vs 3D

### Takeaway
For a beginner the licence cost of every mainstream engine is effectively zero until the game earns real money (Unity free under $200K/yr, Unreal royalty-free under $1M lifetime, Godot free forever, GameMaker $99.99 one-time), so the choice should be driven by target platform, 2D vs 3D, and volume of learning material — not price. Unity still ships about half of all Steam games and dominates mobile; Godot is the fastest-growing option and is solid for PC/mobile 2D but needs paid third-party middleware for consoles; Unreal is strongest for high-end 3D and weakest for mobile/2D and for a solo beginner.

### Inferences
- Licence cost is not a differentiator for a first game: a first game would have to gross >$200K/yr (Unity) or >$1M lifetime (Unreal) before any engine fee applies. The Unity seat cost above the threshold (~$2.3K/yr) is small relative to that revenue.
- GDC survey shares (Unreal-heavy) reflect professional/AAA respondents; Steam release shares (Unity 51%) reflect what small teams actually ship. For a solo beginner the Steam-release data is the more relevant signal.
- Practical mapping for this user: mobile or PC+mobile -> Unity (largest tutorial/asset/ad-SDK ecosystem; Korean-language learning material is abundant); PC-only 2D -> Godot or Unity (GameMaker also viable); high-fidelity 3D PC -> Unreal, but that is the scope a solo beginner should avoid anyway (see section 5). Console ambitions favour Unity/Unreal/GameMaker over Godot because Godot console export is paid third-party middleware.
- Trust risk: Unity's 2023 Runtime Fee episode was fully reversed, but it is the reason a part of the indie community moved to Godot; the growth in Godot Steam releases is consistent with that.

### Gaps
- Could not open Unity's, Epic's or W4's pricing pages directly; W4 Consoles' current price tiers were not found (only a 2023 announcement of Starter/Pro/Enterprise tiers).
- No full-year 2025 engine share for Steam releases from VG Insights was found; latest complete figures are for 2024.
- No reliable quantitative data on engine share among *successful indie* games specifically, or on learning-curve length per engine (only opinion pieces).
- Unverified recollection (not sourced this session): Unity 6 shipped October 2024 and Unity 6.x LTS releases followed in 2025-2026; Spine (Esoteric Software) licences are a one-time ~$69 Essential / ~$379 Professional; mobile ad SDK support (AdMob, AppLovin, Unity LevelPlay) is first-party on Unity and community-plugin-based on Godot. These should be verified before use.

## 2. Art and animation: costs of hiring, asset stores, and styles that look high-quality at low cost

### Takeaway
Art is the largest cash cost for a non-artist solo developer: commissioned 2D game characters typically run from the low hundreds of dollars (static) to $2,500-$7,000 for a fully rigged/animated set, and Korean freelance marketplace list prices for a full-body game character illustration cluster around KRW 300,000-400,000, with Spine rigging/animation and backgrounds priced on top. The way small teams get a "premium" look is not by buying more assets but by choosing a coherent, production-cheap style (stylized/low-poly 3D, strong 2D illustration with limited animation, UI/card-centric presentation, shader/procedural looks) and keeping one art direction throughout.

### Cited Findings

- Per-asset 2D: static character $200-$350 (another guide: character sprite without animation $80-$300); animated character with walk cycle $500-$1,500 (another guide: $200-$600+); complete sprite sheet $800-$5,000. — [Pixune](https://pixune.com/blog/game-art-outsourcing-price/); [Ruxar commissioning guide](https://ruxar.com/game-art-commissions/); [RocketBrush](https://rocketbrush.com/blog/game-art-outsourcing-prices-complete-pricing-breakdown) (search extract did not attribute each figure to a specific one of these pages)

**Asset stores**
- Community view (Unity forum thread): recognizable store assets can get a game labelled an "asset flip"; counter-view in the same thread is that Synty packs are the most recognized yet still used in successful games, and one developer reports no negative reviews mentioning the assets. Forum opinion, not data. — [Unity Discussions thread](https://discussions.unity.com/t/dont-buy-recognizable-asset-store-assets-it-will-be-seen-as-an-asset-flip/928444)
- Steam removed 173 asset-flip titles from one publisher group (Silicon Echo) in September 2017 — OLD (2017), shows Valve acts against mass low-effort flips, not against asset use as such. — [Wikipedia: Asset flip](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_flip)
- The definition of "asset flip" is contested; using purchased assets in an original game is not the same thing. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/in-defense-of-asset-flips-on-steam/)
- Practical advice from indie blogs (promotional, anecdotal): spend custom-art budget on the assets with the biggest impact on the player's experience (main character, key art, UI) and use store assets for the rest. — [Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/blog/fixing-asset-flip-indie-game-monetization)

**Styles and small-team examples**
- Low-poly/stylized production (simplified geometry, flat colour, minimal shaders, stylized animation) lets content be produced faster than photorealism and scales without cost blow-up; the main limiter is artistic coherence — "even simple sprites can look cheap if they aren't stylistically coherent." — [CODA / DEV Community](https://dev.to/evgeniipark/how-a-games-visual-style-affects-production-costs-1i8n)
- Tiny Glade: two developers (Pounce Light), ~two-year development, procedural/custom-tech "cozy diorama" look; launched 23 Sept 2024 with 1,375,441 wishlists after a top-10 Next Fest demo; 616,000 copies in under a month. — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-tiny-glade-built-its-way-to-600k); [Wikipedia: Tiny Glade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny_Glade)
- Balatro: solo developer, started as a hobby December 2021, full-time from June 2023, released February 2024; 1 million copies in under a month. Card/UI-centric presentation with a CRT shader — a case of "shader-driven look + almost no character animation". — [Wikipedia: Balatro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balatro); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/roguelike/solo-dev-behind-roguelike-hit-balatro-expected-to-sell-maybe-10-copies-and-go-back-to-their-it-job-but-they-went-on-to-sell-one-million-copies-instead/)
- Return of the Obra Dinn (solo, 1-bit dithered rendering) and Stardew Valley (solo pixel art) are the standard older examples of a constrained style carrying a solo project — OLD (2018, 2016). — [CODA](https://codaartstudio.medium.com/how-a-games-visual-style-affects-production-costs-a867c50c978d)
- Megabonk (2025, solo, Unity, low-poly retro 3D) and Schedule I (2025, solo, deliberately crude stylized 3D) show that deliberately simple 3D styles sell when the genre/gameplay hook is right. — [Wikipedia: Megabonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabonk); [ScreenRant: best solo indie games 2025](https://screenrant.com/best-solo-indie-games-2025/)

### Inferences
- Budget arithmetic for a small 2D game using the ranges above: 1 hero + 8-10 enemy types with rigged animation sets at even the low-to-mid range ($500-$2,500 each) is roughly $5K-$25K before UI, backgrounds, VFX and key art; a Korean-marketplace route (KRW 300-400K concept + KRW 350K+ Spine rig/idle per character, more for full animation sets) lands in the same order of magnitude, roughly KRW 0.7M-2M per fully animated character. This is why "limited animation" designs (cards, portraits, static illustrations with VFX, tile/board games) are the cost-efficient path to a premium look.
- Animation — not illustration — is the multiplier. Every extra animated state multiplies per-character cost; styles that need few states (card games, visual novels with Live2D/Spine idle only, top-down with simple sprites, physics/procedural animation) keep costs linear.
- Asset-store risk is about coherence and recognizability, not legality: mixing packs with different styles is the common failure. One pack family (or one artist) plus a custom shader/post-processing/lighting pass and custom UI/key art is the usual mitigation.
- Kmong list prices are entry points; commercial-use rights, revision counts and source-file (PSD/part-separated) delivery are typically priced extras, so real contracts will be above list.

### Gaps
- No rigorous freelance rate survey (ArtStation/Upwork/Fiverr aggregate) was found; all figures are vendor guides or individual listings.
- No Korea-specific data found for pixel art, 3D modelling/rigging/animation, UI or VFX outsourcing rates, nor 숨고 (Soomgo) data. No Spine-specific international price list found.
- No sourced detail on Tiny Glade's or Balatro's art budgets; no quantified comparison of production cost by art style from a neutral source.
- Asset-store licence pitfalls (per-seat licences, restrictions on use for AI training, non-exclusive nature of assets) were not researched with sources.

## 3. Generative AI in game art/animation/code (2025-2026): usage, backlash, store policies, copyright, and AI coding for non-programmers

### Takeaway
Gen-AI use in games is now common (roughly a fifth or more of new Steam releases disclose it) but player-facing AI art/voice/cutscenes carry a real, demonstrated review-bombing risk in 2025-2026, and purely AI-generated assets are not copyrightable in the US. The low-risk pattern is: AI for code assistance, brainstorming and internal placeholders (explicitly exempt from Steam's disclosure since January 2026), and human-made (or substantially human-authored) shipped art. AI coding assistants make small 2D prototypes reachable for non-programmers, but shipping a polished paid game still requires someone who can read, debug and own the code.

### Cited Findings
**Steam policy**
- Since January 2024 Steam allows games with AI content subject to a disclosure in the Content Survey (pre-generated vs live-generated content). — [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/95518/valve-will-now-allow-games-with-ai-generated-content-to-be-released-on-steam/index.html)
- Around 16-17 January 2026 Valve rewrote the disclosure form: it is now explicitly about AI-generated content that players consume (in-game art, audio, text, and — per one outlet — marketing/store assets), and not about behind-the-scenes efficiency tools such as code assistants. — [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/steam-updates-ai-disclosure-form-to-specify-that-its-focused-on-ai-generated-content-that-is-consumed-by-players-not-efficiency-tools-used-behind-the-scenes/); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-updates-AI-disclosure-form-requiring-developers-to-report-visible-and-in-game-AI-but-not-background-tools.1206103.0.html)
- Prevalence (figures conflict; different denominators): ~8,000 games had disclosed AI use by H1 2025 vs ~1,000 in 2024, and about one in five new releases carries a disclosure; ~7% of Steam's whole catalogue has a disclosure as of 2026; 40% of games launched in one week of June 2026 disclosed gen-AI (PCGuia, 2026-06-19, single-week figure, unverified). — [Legal Moves law firm](https://legalmoveslawfirm.com/steam-ai-policy/); [Tech-Insider](https://tech-insider.org/steam-ai-disclosure-2026)
- Epic CEO Tim Sweeney has called Steam's AI label policy unnecessary/irresponsible, arguing AI will be part of nearly all production; other storefronts (Epic, PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo) have no comparable public disclosure rule. — [Tech-Insider](https://tech-insider.org/steam-ai-disclosure-2026); [GamesRadar (devs supporting the mandate)](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/steams-required-ai-disclosure-is-the-right-move-devs-say-gen-ai-should-be-called-out-in-games-and-support-valves-store-mandate/)

**Mobile stores**
- Google Play's AI-Generated Content policy targets generative-AI *apps* (developers must prevent offensive/deceptive outputs and provide reporting); it does not expressly require disclosing AI use in an app's description. A claim that Play Console requires declaring AI-generated listing assets since 2 August 2026 comes from a marketing blog and is unverified. — [Google Play Help: AI-Generated Content policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14094294); [Revera legal analysis](https://revera.legal/en/info-centr/news-and-analytical-materials/2114-ii-v-mobilnyx-prilozheniyax-trebovaniya-google-play/amp/); unverified claim: [DEV Community checklist](https://dev.to/gamineai/steam-play-and-app-store-ai-content-disclosure-intake-checklist-for-indie-teams-2026-next-fest-28dk)
- Apple's App Store is reported by secondary sources to have no AI-content disclosure requirement for games. — [Tech-Insider](https://tech-insider.org/steam-ai-disclosure-2026)

**Player backlash cases (2025-2026)**
- Vapor World: Over The Mind (2D soulslike, Early Access): pulled AI-made cutscenes within about a day of launch but still sat at "Mostly Negative" (67% negative). — [Everything Edinburgh](https://everythingedinburgh.com/games/news/vapor-world-steam-ai-cutscene-backlash/)
- Party Animals: a $75,000 AI video contest moved Steam reviews from Very Positive to Mostly Negative in under 24 hours; the studio apologized. — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Party-Animals-hit-with-Steam-review-bombing-as-AI-slop.1299780.0.html); [Pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/gaming/news/recreate-games-apologizes-after-75000-ai-video-contest-triggers-steam-review-bomb-e3f49945-b413-4290-a702-70564e6bbabb/)
- Jurassic World Evolution 3 removed gen-AI portraits after fan pushback; 1666: Amsterdam pledged to replace AI art after demo backlash; The Alters was criticized for undisclosed AI text; Hotel Barcelona added its disclosure five days after release. — [ScreenHub](https://www.screenhub.com.au/news/news/jurassic-world-evolution-3-genai-2671001/); [welcome.ai](https://welcome.ai/content/panache-games-responds-to-community-backlash-by-dropping-ai-art); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/community/topics/the-alters-developer-forgot-to-alter-its-ai-text-before-launch-studio-responds-after-fan-backlash.293798/); [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/hotel-barcelona/generative-ai-disclosure)
- False-positive risk: Shrine's Legacy received negative reviews accusing it of AI use that the developer says it did not use — hand-made art that "looks AI" can also be attacked. — [search extract; see WatchMojo roundup](https://www.watchmojo.com/articles/10-games-that-got-backlash-for-ai-use) (attribution approximate)

**Copyright**
- US Copyright Office Part 2 report (January 2025): copyright requires human authorship; prompts alone generally do not make the user the author of the output; human selection/arrangement/modification of AI material can be protected, but protection covers only the human contribution; AI as an assisting tool does not defeat copyright. — [Jenner & Block client alert](https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/client-alerts/client-alert-us-copyright-office-issues-report-on-copyrightability-of-works-incorporating-ai-generated-material); [DLA Piper, March 2025](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-la/insights/publications/2025/03/copyrightability-of-genai-outputs-in-the-us-key-developments)
- Thaler v. Perlmutter: D.C. Circuit affirmed on 18 March 2025 that a work generated autonomously by AI cannot be registered; the US Supreme Court denied certiorari on 2 March 2026, leaving that ruling in place. — [Mayer Brown, 2026-03-11](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/supreme-court-denies-review-in-ai-authorship-case); [Munck Wilson](https://www.munckwilson.com/news-insights/supreme-court-denies-certiorari-in-thaler-v-perlmutter-human-authorship-still-required/)

**AI coding assistants for a non-programmer**
- Vendor/blog consensus (2025-2026): "vibe coding" works for small 2D games with clear mechanics (platformers, puzzle, idle, visual novels, simple RPGs) and collapses the idea-to-playable-prototype gap; it hits limits with multiplayer, performance and 3D asset pipelines. — [Ziva: vibe coding games](https://ziva.sh/blogs/vibe-coding-games); [Cinevva signal, March 2026](https://app.cinevva.com/signals/2026-03-13-vibe-coding-new-game-jam); [Nilo](https://nilo.io/articles/vibegame-engine)
- Critical views: non-programmers cannot effectively debug AI-generated code when it breaks; "anything you ship to paying users needs someone who can own the code"; hands-on accounts say most effort still goes into tuning, testing and deciding what is fun. — [Cendyne, Aug 2025](https://cendyne.dev/posts/2025-08-21-vibe-codings-hype-for-non-developers-is-over.html); [ShiftMag](https://shiftmag.dev/?p=9255); [KDnuggets](https://www.kdnuggets.com/?p=201205)
- Steam explicitly exempts code-assist tools from AI disclosure (January 2026 form). — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/steam-updates-ai-disclosure-form-to-specify-that-its-focused-on-ai-generated-content-that-is-consumed-by-players-not-efficiency-tools-used-behind-the-scenes/)

### Inferences
- Risk asymmetry for a "visual quality" positioning: disclosed AI art attracts backlash on Steam, undisclosed AI art that is later discovered attracts worse backlash, and AI-only assets cannot be protected against copying. For a game whose selling point is art, shipping human-made art (AI at most for ideation/placeholders) is the defensible choice on PC. Mobile stores currently impose no disclosure and mobile audiences have shown less organized backlash, but that is an absence of evidence in these results, not a measured finding.
- Korea-based developers selling globally are governed in practice by store policy plus US/EU copyright positions; an AI-generated key character that cannot be registered is a weak foundation for an IP-driven monetization plan (merch, sequels, licensing).
- AI coding makes "non-programmer ships a small, well-scoped single-player 2D game" plausible if the person invests in learning to read and debug the code along the way; it does not remove the need for design iteration, playtesting and performance work, and it argues further against multiplayer/3D-heavy scope.

### Gaps
- No controlled data on sales impact of an AI disclosure label (only anecdotes of review-bombing).
- Apple's and Google's exact current guideline text on AI assets in games was not retrieved from primary pages.
- Korea-specific legal position on AI-generated works (Korea Copyright Commission guidance, 2023-2025 "AI Basic Act") was not researched.
- No verified case of a non-programmer shipping a commercially successful Unity/Godot game with AI assistance alone was found.
- Third-party IP infringement risk from AI outputs (training-data lawsuits) was not researched in this session.

## 4. Timelines, budgets, base rates, and the "make small games first" evidence

### Takeaway
The base rate is harsh: of 20,282 Steam games released in 2025 only 608 (about 3%) reached 1,000 reviews, roughly two-thirds earned under $1,000, and about 75% of Steam studios never release a second game. The best-supported advice (Chris Zukowski and VG Insights data) is to ship small games in months, not years — later games earn more on average, and several sub-nine-month games have grossed $150K+ — rather than betting 2-4 years on a first "dream game".

### Cited Findings
**Base rates**
- 20,282 games released on Steam in 2025; 608 reached 1,000+ user reviews (2.99%), the highest rate of the last four years (2024: 2.44%; 2023: 2.56%). (Zukowski's annual tally.) — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/a-terrifying-20-282-games-were-released-on-steam-in-2025-and-just-608-managed-to-get-1-000-reviews-expert-finds-we-might-be-in-a-bit-of-an-indie-golden-age/)
- Gamalytic estimates for 2025 Steam releases (measured in autumn 2025, estimates not Valve data): 65.9% earned under $1,000 (a Chinese-language report cites 55.3% — conflict); 47.4% sold under 100 copies; ~40% did not earn back the $100 Steam Direct fee; the bottom 30% averaged $37 total. — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/over-5-000-games-released-on-steam-this-year-didnt-make-enough-money-to-recover-the-usd100-fee-to-put-a-game-on-valves-store-research-estimates); [Tech4Gamers](https://tech4gamers.com/40-of-steam-games-in-2025-didnt-even-earn-100-dollars/)
- Valve's own figure: 5,863 games earned over $100,000 on Steam in 2025 (includes older titles' 2025 earnings as far as is known; Valve did not specify). — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/valve-shares-more-than-5-800-games-on-steam-earned-over-usd100-000-in-2025)
- OLD (2019 cohort, reported 2022): median paid Steam game lifetime gross about $1,136; $10,000 is roughly the 68th percentile; Zukowski advised aiming for the 80th percentile (~$34,000) with a first game. — [Game World Observer, 2022-11-29](https://gameworldobserver.com/2022/11/29/median-indie-game-earnings-steam-barely-over-1000)
- Zukowski's "Real Steam" benchmark: about $150,000 gross within six to nine months of release; 74% of games that reach it do so in their first three months; a game that does not reach ~250 reviews in its first month will probably not get there. — [Yahoo Tech / PC Gamer syndication](https://tech.yahoo.com/general/articles/marketing-guy-invents-concept-real-195115142.html); [Yahoo Tech / GamesRadar syndication](https://tech.yahoo.com/deals/articles/steam-expert-says-devs-250-173133971.html)
- Steam's visibility thresholds penalize tiny launches: reaching 10 reviews takes roughly 300 copies sold (Zukowski). Date of the underlying article unclear, likely pre-2024. — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/analytics/item-3827)

**First game vs later games**
- OLD (2023): Zukowski's "missing middle" article states that 75% of studios on Steam have released one and only one game, and attributes it to developers skipping small/medium games and quitting after a failed large first project (his interpretation). A related figure quoted from his material: ~38,000 studios released one game vs ~7,920 that released two (exact page not confirmed). — [How To Market A Game, 2023-09-28](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/09/28/the-missing-middle-in-game-development/)
- VG Insights (as compiled by a secondary site, 2026): average gross revenue ~$120,000 for debut games, ~$168,000 for second games, ~$209,000 for third games. Averages (not medians), heavily skewed by hits; original report date not confirmed. — [VoxBooster compilation](https://voxbooster.com/blog/indie-game-statistics-2026/)
- Zukowski (Inside Conference talk, summarized 2025-2026) recommends small games as a ladder, citing developers who made games in under nine months that earned $150,000+ and some reaching nearly 200,000 wishlists; he compares it to Mark Duplass's "$1,000 movie" advice. — [Freaking Cool Indies](https://freakingcoolindies.com/chris-z-teaches-indie-devs-to-build-an-audience-heres-how-he-built-his/)
- Zukowski also argues a "hidden middle" of games quietly making hundreds of thousands of dollars for small devs exists but is filtered out of most players' view. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/between-the-total-failures-and-the-stardew-valley-sized-hits-expert-says-its-the-hidden-gems-quietly-making-hundreds-of-thousands-of-dollars-for-small-devs-that-show-steam-is-doing-okay/)
- He attributes many "great game nobody bought" cases to genre mismatch with the Steam audience, frequently on first games; marketing cannot rescue a genre Steam players do not buy. — [Xsolla Accelerator summary of How To Market A Game](https://accelerator.xsolla.com/blog/howtomarketagame)

**Timelines and budgets (weakly sourced; blogs, not surveys)**
- Small complete 2D game: 3-6 months full-time or 9-18 months part-time; mid-size solo project: 1-2 years; one blog cites ~18 months as the average indie ship time without naming data. — [gtstu](https://gtstu.com/how-long-does-it-take-to-make-an-indie-game/); [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/indie-game-development-costs)
- Solo developers typically spend $5K-$50K in cash, with unpaid labour being the real budget; doing your own art saves cash but roughly doubles development time (blog estimate). — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/indie-game-development-costs); [VSquad](https://vsquad.art/blog/articles/indie-game-budgets-what-it-really-costs-to-build-a-game/)
- Hit examples for calibration: Megabonk — solo, development began August 2024, released 2025 (about a year), 1M copies in two weeks; Balatro — solo, ~2 years part-time then ~8 months full-time; Tiny Glade — two experienced devs, ~2 years. — [Wikipedia: Megabonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabonk); [Wikipedia: Balatro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balatro); [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-tiny-glade-built-its-way-to-600k)

### Inferences
- A first-time developer should plan financially for the median outcome (under $1,000 gross) and treat anything above ~$10K as a good first result; "commercial success" on a first title is a low-single-digit-percent event even in favourable genres.
- The evidence for "small games first" is directional rather than causal (later games earn more partly because survivors are better developers), but the cost of being wrong is asymmetric: a 6-9 month game that fails costs months; a 3-year first game that fails ends most studios (the 75% one-and-done figure).
- Part-time development roughly triples calendar time (3-6 months full-time vs 9-18 months part-time per the blog range), which matters if the user keeps a job.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 dataset was found giving median development time of Steam games, or hit rate broken down by number of prior releases; the Zukowski 75% figure is from 2023 and the VG Insights first/second/third-game figures are via a secondary compiler.
- No reliable survey of budgets of *successful* small indies was found (VG Insights' indie budget reports were not surfaced).
- First-time mobile developer survival/earnings base rates were not researched (out of scope / covered by other researchers).

## 5. Scope guidance: feasible game types for a solo beginner vs traps

### Takeaway
Genre choice is the single biggest controllable success factor on Steam: in 2025, hit rates (share reaching 1,000 reviews) ranged from ~0.2% for 2D platformers to 5% for roguelike deckbuilders and 20-35% for job-simulator and open-world survival-craft games. For a solo beginner with 6-12 months, the feasible high-polish targets are small, systems-driven, single-player games with low animation/content needs (roguelike deckbuilder-lite, horror short, idle/incremental, simulation/management-lite, cozy builder), while online multiplayer, open-world, MMO and content-heavy RPG scopes are traps regardless of how well those genres sell.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 Steam hit rates (≥1,000 reviews) by genre, Zukowski: roguelike deckbuilders 11 of 212 (5.1%); simulation 4.1%; horror 3.2%; overall 2.99%. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/a-terrifying-20-282-games-were-released-on-steam-in-2025-and-just-608-managed-to-get-1-000-reviews-expert-finds-we-might-be-in-a-bit-of-an-indie-golden-age/)
- 2D platformers: 3 of 1,658 released in 2025 reached 1,000 reviews (~0.18%); puzzle is heavily over-supplied (4,022 puzzle games released in 2025; hit rate not found). — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/a-terrifying-20-282-games-were-released-on-steam-in-2025-and-just-608-managed-to-get-1-000-reviews-expert-finds-we-might-be-in-a-bit-of-an-indie-golden-age/); [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-many-games-release-on-steam)
- Top genres by hit rate in one 2026 compilation: job simulator 34.7%, open-world survival craft 20.8% (2024 figure from another summary: OWSC 24.47%, 2D platformer 0.25%). Genre denominators rely on loosely applied Steam tags. — [PokeIndie genre study 2026](https://pokeindie.com/blog/game-genre-study-2026-which-genres-are-worth-building); [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/indie-game-marketing-timeline)
- H1 2024: only 232 of 8,000+ Steam releases reached 1,000 reviews; article lists top genres and trends. — [Game World Observer, 2024-07-18](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/07/18/top-game-genres-on-steam-first-half-2024-key-trends)
- AI-assisted development "quickly hits limits when you need multiplayer, strong performance, or export-ready 3D assets". — [Nilo](https://nilo.io/articles/vibegame-engine)
- Solo hits in 2024-2025 are small-scope, systems-driven, single-player or simple co-op: Balatro (poker roguelike deckbuilder), Megabonk (3D survivors-like roguelike), Schedule I (crude-styled sim). — [Wikipedia: Balatro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balatro); [Wikipedia: Megabonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabonk); [ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/best-solo-indie-games-2025/)
- Zukowski's small-game examples: games built in under nine months grossing $150K+. — [Freaking Cool Indies](https://freakingcoolindies.com/chris-z-teaches-indie-devs-to-build-an-audience-heres-how-he-built-his/)

### Inferences
- The high-hit-rate genres split into two groups. Feasible for a solo beginner: roguelike deckbuilder / card-and-systems games (5.1%; art is illustration + UI, minimal animation), short horror (3.2%; atmosphere, lighting and sound over asset count), idle/incremental and small simulation/management games (simulation 4.1%). Not feasible: open-world survival craft (20.8% hit rate but requires 3D open world, crafting, usually multiplayer — a team-scale scope).
- Traps to state explicitly for the report: (a) MMO / live-service / PvP online multiplayer (netcode, servers, anti-cheat, needs concurrent player base from day one); (b) open-world and content-heavy RPGs (content volume scales linearly with art cost from section 2); (c) 2D platformers and generic puzzle games on Steam (feasible to build, but ~0.2% hit rate / extreme oversupply — a market trap rather than a production trap); (d) story-heavy games needing many animated characters/cutscenes.
- "High polish in 6-12 months" is achievable only when polish effort is concentrated: one core loop, a small number of screens, replayability from systems (roguelike structure) rather than hand-made content, and juice/feedback/UI polish rather than asset count.
- On mobile, the equivalent feasible set is hybrid-casual/puzzle/idle with ads+IAP, but success there depends on UA economics (section 6), not just build quality.

### Gaps
- Zukowski's hit rates for idle/incremental and "cozy" games in 2025 were not found; his full 2025 genre table was not accessible.
- No sourced, systematic mapping of genre -> typical solo dev time; the feasibility classification above is inference from scope characteristics plus hit rates.
- Hit rate by 1,000 reviews is a Steam metric; no comparable genre hit-rate data for mobile was gathered.

## 6. Marketing playbook for small indies (2025-2026), PC and mobile

### Takeaway
On Steam the proven low-budget process is: pick a genre Steam buys -> publish the Coming Soon page as soon as there is a good trailer/screenshots (6-12 months pre-launch) -> release a public demo early and push it to streamers -> enter online festivals -> use Steam Next Fest once, late, with momentum -> launch with roughly 7,000-10,000+ wishlists. Wishlists already banked before Next Fest are the strongest predictor of Next Fest results (median gain 806 in Feb 2026). On mobile, the equivalent is a 4-12 week soft launch in cheap tier-2 markets, with go/no-go decided on D1/D7 retention against CPI before any scaling spend.

### Cited Findings
**Steam page and wishlists**
- Most guides recommend opening the Coming Soon page 6-12 months before launch (some say minimum 3-6 months); Steam requires at least two weeks in Coming Soon before release. — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-coming-soon-page-guide); [The Game Marketer](https://www.thegamemarketer.com/insight-posts/how-to-publish-your-game-on-steam-guide)
- Zukowski's long-standing rule of thumb is ~7,000 wishlists before launch to have a chance at the Popular Upcoming list; a June 2026 update reportedly says the bar has risen, with the median game on that list at 13,270 wishlists on one day that month. Valve says there is no hard threshold; Zukowski: "There is no magic number." (Via secondary site; verify on howtomarketagame.com.) — [Vast.gg guide](https://vast.gg/guides/how-to-get-steam-wishlists); [games.gg](https://games.gg/news/steam-popular-upcoming-wishlist-guide/)
- Other guides cite 7,000-10,000 wishlists as a realistic launch target and 30,000-50,000 as a strong-revenue tier. — [Althera Games](https://altheragames.com/en/blog/steam-wishlist-guide); [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/how-many-wishlists-to-launch)
- Reality check on organic reach: one analysis puts the median game at about 200 wishlists from a week of marketing effort; a developer benchmark quoted by Game Developer treats ~20 wishlists/day as an "average good indie game". — [Recognizing Patterns](https://recognizingpatterns.substack.com/p/i-told-investors-this-game-would); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/are-these-really-the-ugly-truths-for-indie-publishers-2)

**Demo and Steam Next Fest**
- Zukowski's February 2026 Next Fest survey (182 games): median game gained 806 wishlists during the fest; bottom third under ~400; top 5% gained 13,000+. Pre-fest wishlist count was the strongest predictor of wishlists earned (Spearman 0.825). — [Steam Page Analyzer summary](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-to-get-steam-wishlists); primary: [How To Market A Game benchmarks](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/)
- Demo timing: games whose demo had been public for more than a month before the fest earned about 2.5x the wishlists of games that first dropped the demo during the fest (1.4x for demos out weeks before). — [Ziva summary](https://ziva.sh/blogs/steam-next-fest-2026); [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-to-get-steam-wishlists)
- In Zukowski's July 2026 post, 82% of games entering with 2,000+ wishlists earned 3,000+ during the fest vs 27% of games with 2,000 or fewer (table definitions unclear in extract; verify). — [How To Market A Game, 2026-07-14](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/07/14/games-that-used-momentum-for-steam-next-fest-success/)
- A game can participate in Next Fest once, so guides advise entering only with momentum, close to launch. — [Cinevva Next Fest strategy](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/steam-next-fest-strategy)
- Demo-to-wishlist conversion of 25-35% is "average" and 60%+ outstanding according to a studio blog (low weight). — [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/steam-next-fest-demo-optimization-wishlists)
- Case: Tiny Glade reached the Next Fest top 10 (June 2024) and launched with 1.38M wishlists. — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-tiny-glade-built-its-way-to-600k)

**Channels**
- Attributed to Zukowski (interviews, via secondary site): festivals and streamer coverage together produce roughly 90% of new Steam wishlist adds. A secondary ranking puts online festivals and Steam front-page featuring first, then TikTok, streamers and Reddit, with press and Twitter/X lower; no cross-game dataset ranks channels. — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-to-get-steam-wishlists)
- OLD (2023 talk): Zukowski said Twitter works poorly unless the game is "Twitter-friendly", while festivals, streamers and TikTok were working. — [Game Devs Journey summary](https://gamedevsjourney.substack.com/p/how-to-market-your-game-on-steam)
- Unity's summary of Zukowski's advice: genre fit and the Steam page come before any promotion; use traffic from earlier games to promote new ones. — [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/tips-strategies-for-marketing-indie-games)
- Top-of-market deflation: average wishlists for top-100 indie titles fell from ~1.2M (pre-2022 reveals) to ~0.7M (2025-2026 reveals). — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/steam-wishlists-by-the-numbers-what-actually-gets-wishlisted)

**Paid marketing benchmarks**
- Paid ads cost per wishlist: about $1-2 is acceptable for a well-targeted campaign; stop or rework above $3. One Reddit-ads case study (Danchi Days): ~$7,000 spent for ~3,600 wishlists, $1.79-$1.94 each. — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/indie-game-marketing-budget); [Melodic Ambient / Danchi Days write-up](https://buttondown.com/melodicambient/archive/danchi-days-3600-wishlists-through-reddit-ads-was)
- Marketing budget as a share of development budget: vendor benchmarks vary from 20-30% to 25-50%; one analyst argues extra development runway usually beats a bigger campaign for small indies. Suggested ad test: $10-20/day for two to three weeks before committing more. Meta gaming CPC reportedly rose from ~$0.96 (early 2025) to $3.27 (January 2026). — [Theory-Craft Marketing](https://www.theorycraftmarketing.com/how-to-structure-your-marketing-budget-for-a-game-launch); [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/video-game-advertising-costs)

**Mobile**
- Soft launch: typically a 4-12 week limited release in controlled lower-cost markets (commonly Canada, Philippines, Australia, New Zealand); one guide's rule: if D1 retention is below 25% on Android, iterate before spending on acquisition. — [Game Growth Advisor soft-launch guide, Dec 2025](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2025-12-16-mobile-soft-launch-complete-guide/)
- CPI: casual games averaged $1.41 on iOS and $0.14 on Android (Liftoff/Singular, Feb 2024-Feb 2025, includes hypercasual); Adjust reports global gaming CPI up 30% YoY in 2025 to $0.56; hybrid-casual Android CPI roughly doubled from $0.54 (2024) to $0.95 (2025). — [Liftoff 2025 Casual Gaming Apps Report](https://liftoff.ai/?p=34267); [GameDev Reports on Adjust Gaming App Insights 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-gaming-app-insights-report)
- Retention: median D1 ~22%, D7 ~4%; top-10% games ~40% D1 and ~12% D7 (GameAnalytics data via a January 2026 compilation); Adjust's 2025 all-genre D1 is 27%. — [InvestGame: Mobile retention benchmarks 2026 (PDF)](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-20-Mobile_retention_benchmarks_2026.pdf); [GameDev Reports on Adjust](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-gaming-app-insights-report)
- Vendor (unsourced) 2026 CPI tiers for hybrid-casual: $2-4 in tier-1 countries, $1-2 in tier-2. — [FoxData](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/2026-mobile-game-user-acquisition-cost-benchmarks-how-much-should-you-spend/)

### Inferences
- Sequenced PC playbook for this user (derived from the findings): (1) validate genre against hit-rate data; (2) build a vertical slice with final-quality art for the first 10 minutes; (3) Steam page live 6-12 months before launch, the day there is a real trailer; (4) public demo ≥1 month before the chosen Next Fest, emailed to mid-size streamers in the genre; (5) apply to every relevant online festival (free); (6) Next Fest as the last big beat before launch; (7) launch only when wishlists are in the ~7-10K+ range (the bar appears to be rising toward ~13K for Popular Upcoming in 2026) — or accept a small launch and treat the game as a learning release.
- A visually strong game is advantaged in exactly the channels that work (short-form video, Reddit, festival capsule art, streamer thumbnails); this is where the art budget pays for itself in marketing. But the data (806 median Next Fest wishlists; ~200 wishlists/week typical) shows that visuals alone do not carry a game in a weak genre.
- Cash marketing budget for a first small game can be near zero to low thousands of dollars: festivals and streamer outreach are free; paid ads at $1-2 per wishlist only make sense once the page converts and should be tested at $10-20/day.
- Mobile is structurally different: discovery is pay-to-play (CPI vs LTV), so a first-time solo dev's realistic mobile plan is a cheap soft launch to read retention, then either a publisher (who funds UA) or staying small/organic; a premium-quality art style does not by itself lower CPI.

### Gaps

## 7. Team structure options: solo + contractors, co-founder artist, revenue-share collaborations

### Takeaway
For someone whose own strength is not yet defined, the lowest-risk structure is solo ownership with paid work-for-hire contractors (clear IP assignment, fixed scope), because revenue-share collaborations mostly fail to ship and a co-founder relationship without vesting and a written agreement is the classic way first projects die. A co-founder artist is the highest-upside option for a visual-quality goal, but only with a signed agreement covering split, IP, vesting and exit.

### Cited Findings
- "The overwhelming majority of indie rev-share projects never reach a sellable state"; there is no industry standard for splits, vesting or structure, which is why the written agreement matters; core terms are the split, IP assignment, vesting and exit. — [Legal Moves: indie game rev-share agreements](https://legalmoveslawfirm.com/indie-game-rev-share/); [Legal Moves service page](https://legalmoveslawfirm.com/services/revenue-share-agreement)
- Vesting: each founder should sign a shareholder agreement with a vesting schedule (rule of thumb ~25% per completed year) so that a departing member forfeits unvested equity; rev-share percentages can alternatively vest on project milestones. — [Game Developer: From indie dev to CEO — 10 indie pitfalls](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/from-indie-dev-to-ceo-10-indie-pitfalls); [Legal Moves](https://legalmoveslawfirm.com/indie-game-rev-share/)
- Expected value is small: Steam takes 30% before any split, and a 10% share of a median-earning indie game is worth a few hundred dollars — "unshipped points are lottery tickets, not compensation." — [Bugnet: work-for-hire vs revenue share](https://bugnet.io/blog/work-for-hire-vs-revenue-share-for-indie-teams); [Li Ortega: hidden cost of revenue-share deals](https://www.liortega.com/post/the-hidden-cost-of-revenue-share-deals)
- Do not split 100% of revenue among members; retain a share for the company to fund further development (forum advice, date old). — [GameDev.net thread](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/675199-revenue-sharing-how-to-do-it-how-not-to-do-it/5273911/)
- Team-dynamics problems recur: differing financial needs and commitment levels; rev-share collaborators (e.g. composers) deprioritize the project against paid work. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/from-indie-dev-to-ceo-10-indie-pitfalls); [Ninichi Music](https://ninichimusic.com/blog/pros-cons-of-revenue-share-deals-with-composers)
- Written contracts for collaborators and contractors are described as essential to prevent later disputes over IP and money. — [Wayline: game development contracts](https://www.wayline.io/blog/game-development-contracts-legal-licensing-indie-devs)
- Reference structures among recent hits: Balatro and Megabonk were solo; Tiny Glade was a two-person team with complementary senior skills (technical artist + rendering engineer). — [Wikipedia: Balatro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balatro); [Wikipedia: Megabonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabonk); [Wikipedia: Tiny Glade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny_Glade)

### Inferences
- Option comparison for this user: (a) Solo + paid contractors — cash cost up front (section 2 figures), full IP ownership, no dependency on a partner's motivation; requires the user to be the designer/programmer/producer. Best default. (b) Co-founder artist — no cash cost and the best route to a distinctive look, but demands equity, a vesting agreement and compatible commitment; hard for a beginner with no track record to attract a strong artist. (c) Pure rev-share with strangers — cheapest on paper, worst completion odds; skilled artists rarely accept it from a first-timer. A hybrid (reduced paid rate + small rev-share bonus tied to milestones) is the common compromise.
- Given a median first-game outcome under $1,000, any rev-share offer is honestly worth near zero in expectation; paying modest fixed fees for a small, well-defined asset list is both fairer and more likely to produce finished work.
- For a Korea-based developer hiring internationally, the contract must explicitly assign copyright (work-for-hire doctrine differs by country) and grant commercial-use rights — marketplace gigs often price commercial rights separately.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on completion rates of rev-share projects or on solo vs team success rates on Steam; all sources are legal/blog advice.
- Korea-specific legal structure (개인사업자 vs 법인, 외주 계약서 표준, 저작권 양도 조항) was not researched.

## 8. Funding: publishers, grants, crowdfunding, Epic MegaGrants, Korean support programmes

### Takeaway
Self-funding is the norm (50% of GDC's Nov 2026 respondents say their company is mainly self-funded vs 15% on publisher deals), publishers now expect a near-final-quality vertical slice plus traction rather than an idea, and video-game Kickstarter is small ($26M across 443 funded projects in 2025, ~68% of campaigns fail). For a Korea-based beginner the most relevant non-dilutive money is KOCCA's Korea Indie Game Dev Camp (individuals up to KRW 85M in staged grants) and, for Unreal projects, Epic MegaGrants, which is active with two application cycles in 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Self-funding and publishers**
- GDC November 2026 report: 50% of respondents say their company's predominant operational funding is self-funding, 15% publishing deals (35% self-funding among AAA workers). The January 2026 edition reported 20% relying on publishing deals/project financing. Surveys may not be directly comparable. — [GDC State of the Game Industry: November 2026](https://gdconf.com/state-of-the-game-industry-november-2026/); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/gdc-survey-captures-changing-game-professional-views-of-ai-adoption-labor-and-mental-health/)
- Publishers in 2025-2026 typically engage only once a team has a playable build or vertical slice with a clear game loop; the market is described as "hypercompetition" at end-2025. — [Game World Observer, 2026-01-29](https://gameworldobserver.com/2026/01/29/the-market-finally-entered-a-phase-of-hypercompetition-digital-vortexs-inna-gosteva-about-the-situation-as-of-the-end-of-2025)
- A pitching agency that says it handled 500+ publisher pitches in 2025 argues the slice must look and play close to the final game (polished UI, few placeholders). Self-interested source. — [Beck Interactive Partners](https://beckinteractivepartners.substack.com/p/the-vertical-slice-meta-part-1-we)
- A short, polished ~10-minute slice beats a long rough one (publisher opinion). — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/are-these-really-the-ugly-truths-for-indie-publishers-2)
- No More Robots signs at concept or vertical-slice stage and weighs developer personality alongside quality. — [daily.dev summary](https://app.daily.dev/posts/no-more-robots-on-indie-publishing-strategy-hpsfk6elk)
- Typical deal shape described in one guide: around a 30% publisher share with a recoupment phase (publisher recovers its spend first). Guide's sourcing is weak; verify. — [games.gg](https://games.gg/news/indie-game-publisher-pitching-deals-self-publishing/)

**Crowdfunding**
- Kickstarter video games in 2025 (Ico Partners' Thomas Bidaux): about $26 million raised across 443 funded projects, flat vs 2024; 55 campaigns above $100K and 11 above $500K. Top 2025 video game campaign: Elestrals Awakened, >$1.3M from >9,000 backers. — [GameDiscoverCo: What worked for video game crowdfunding in 2025?](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-worked-for-video-game-crowdfunding); [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/kickstarter-for-indie-games)
- About 68% of video-game campaigns fail to reach their goal (≈32% success, stable over the years); historical total ~$377M across 5,526 funded video-game campaigns since 2009 (avg ~$68.2K); February is the best month (35% success), December the worst (28%). — [GameDev Reports: Kickstarter in 2025 and before](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/kickstarter-in-2025-and-before); [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/kickstarter-for-indie-games)
- 2024 set a record for the number of successful video-game campaigns on Kickstarter; but the Games category is dominated by tabletop ($220M of $270M pledged in 2024, 83%; tabletop success rate 80%). — [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/kickstarter-2024-set-a-record-for); [Kickstarter update](https://updates.kickstarter.com/kickstarter-biggest-platform-for-games/)
- GameDiscoverCo's 2024 analysis: a playable demo at campaign launch is now effectively required to win influencer coverage and backer trust. — [Game Industry Library summary](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/gamediscoverco-making-a-successful-video-game-kickstarter-in-2024/insights)

**Epic MegaGrants**
- Active, not paused: 2026 has two cycles — Cycle 1 submissions closed 20 March 2026 (notices 15-19 June); Cycle 2 submissions 29 June-4 September 2026, review 5 September-4 December, notices 7-11 December 2026. Epic's February 2026 recap says the programme has funded 2,100+ projects in 102 countries. — [Epic MegaGrants page](https://www.unrealengine.com/megagrants); [Epic: MegaGrants 2025 recap, 2026-02-03](https://www.unrealengine.com/news/epic-megagrants-2025-recap-first-submissions-are-now-open)

- KOCCA "2026 Korea Indie Game Dev Camp" (코리아 인디게임 데브캠프): KRW 6 billion total; company and individual tracks; staged development grants — for individuals: stage 1 (plan) 60 projects x KRW 10M, stage 2 (early build) 35 x KRW 20M, stage 3 (prototype) 20 x KRW 25M, stage 4 (vertical slice) 10 x KRW 30M; cumulative maximum KRW 85M per individual project and KRW 140M per company project; eligible: businesses under 7 years old or pre-founders; funds usable for labour, outsourcing, software and marketing; 2026 application window 23 February-23 March 2026. — [THE VC grant listing](https://thevc.kr/grants/f0751169e1e40ce61ee1dd78); [한국강사신문](https://www.lecturernews.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=197383)

Aimed at companies. — [국민일보](https://www.kmib.co.kr/article/view.asp?arcid=0029419463); [S저널](https://www.s-journal.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=38837)

### Inferences
- Realistic funding stack for this user: personal savings/income for a small first game -> KOCCA Dev Camp individual track (next call presumably around February-March 2027, based on the 2026 calendar) -> optional Kickstarter only if a demo and an audience already exist -> publisher pitch only after a polished vertical slice with wishlist traction. A first-time developer with no shipped title and no slice is very unlikely to get a publisher deal in the current market.
- Kickstarter at ~$26M/year for all video games is tiny relative to Steam; it works as a marketing beat for games with an existing audience, not as primary funding for an unknown first-timer (68% fail).
- The KOCCA staged structure (plan -> early build -> prototype -> vertical slice) mirrors exactly what publishers ask for, so it doubles as a discipline framework; KRW 85M (~$60K) would cover the art-outsourcing budget estimated in section 2 several times over.
- MegaGrants only helps if the project uses Unreal (or related Epic ecosystem), which conflicts with the beginner-friendly small-2D scope recommended elsewhere.
- Note the KOCCA AI-game track (KRW 2.7B, new in 2026): public funding in Korea is actively encouraging AI use in games, in tension with the global player backlash documented in section 3.
