# Steam (PC) as a target market for a first-time indie developer (2025-2026)

> Research date: 2026-11-18. Method caveat: in this session direct page fetches were blocked (howtomarketagame.com, newsletter.gamediscover.co, gamedevreports.substack.com, steampageanalyzer.com and others did not resolve), so every figure below comes from web-search result summaries of the linked pages, not from reading the pages in full. Numbers attributed to Zukowski, GameDiscoverCo, Gamalytic, Valve etc. should be spot-checked against the linked originals before being quoted in a final report. Third-party revenue figures (Gamalytic, VG Insights, Alinea, GameDiscoverCo) are estimates, not Valve data. Anything marked "background knowledge" was not verified in this session.

## 1. Release volume and revenue distribution (2023-2026)

### Takeaway
Steam supply is at a record and still accelerating (about 14k games in 2023, 18.5k in 2024, 19-20.5k in 2025, and 2026 had already passed all of 2025 by mid-October 2026), while the typical game earns almost nothing: roughly 40% of 2025 releases grossed under $100, about two thirds under $1,000, and only around 8-10% reach $50k-$100k. The absolute number of winners is growing, though: Valve says 5,863 games earned over $100k in 2025 (a record), up from about 3,000 in 2020.

**Releases per year**
- SteamDB counts cited in December 2025: 14,111 games in 2023, 18,558 in 2024, 19,008 in 2025 (snapshot taken before year end; another same-period snapshot gave 19,112) — [GamingOnLinux, Dec 2025](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/12/over-19000-games-have-released-on-steam-in-2025-with-nearly-half-seeing-fewer-than-10-reviews)
- Final-year 2025 tallies differ by tracker: 20,282 (VG Insights data used by Chris Zukowski), 20,558 (GameDiscoverCo), 20,353 (Gamalytic); SteamDB's 2025 figure grew by about 1,400 between December 2025 and June 2026 through retroactive additions. "More than 19,000" is the conservative framing — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-many-games-release-on-steam); primary post: [Zukowski, 2026-01-08](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/01/08/how-many-games-were-released-in-2025/)
- 2025 was the highest annual release total in Steam's history at that point — [Generation Amiga, 2025-12-27](https://www.generationamiga.com/2025/12/27/from-14k-to-19k-steam-game-launches-hit-all-time-high-in-2025/)

- 2026: the week of August 24-30, 2026 had 720 releases, the first week ever above 700 (ICO Partners' Thomas Bidaux); a later report cites 1,656 launches in a single week, more than doubling the earlier record (exact week not confirmed) — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/25520/new-steam-releases-hit-record-high-of-720-in-one-week-averaging-103-per-day); [Inven Global follow-up](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/28193/weekly-steam-releases-surpass-1656-more-than-doubling-previous-record)

- Almost half of all games released in 2025 received fewer than 10 user reviews (SteamDB data, Dec 2025) — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/more-than-19-000-games-launched-on-steam-this-year-but-almost-half-have-fewer-than-10-reviews); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/steam-earned-usd16b-in-2025-but-nearly-half-of-19-000-games-got-under-10-reviews)

- Valve (GDC, March 2026): 5,863 games earned more than $100,000 on Steam in 2025, a record; just over 3,000 did in 2020. The count covers revenue earned during 2025 by any game, including titles released before 2025 — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/valve-says-over-5000-games-made-over-usd100k-on-steam-last-year); [Game World Observer, 2026-03-11](https://gameworldobserver.com/2026/03/11/in-2025-nearly-6000-games-earned-over-100000-on-steam-a-record)

**Median / percentile revenue (third-party estimates)**
- Gamalytic (reported Oct 2025, partial-year data): 40% of 2025 Steam releases grossed less than $100 (i.e., did not recoup the Steam Direct fee; over 5,000 games); 8,388 games (65.9%) earned under $1,000; median revenue per 2025 game $318; only 23.4% made over $5k — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/over-5-000-games-released-on-steam-this-year-didnt-make-enough-money-to-recover-the-usd100-fee-to-put-a-game-on-valves-store-research-estimates); [Tech4Gamers](https://tech4gamers.com/40-of-steam-games-in-2025-didnt-even-earn-100-dollars/); [Spilled.gg](https://spilled.gg/5000-steam-games-2025-failed-make-back-100-submission-fee)
- Year-end 2025 analysis: median revenue of a 2025 release was $249, the second-lowest since 2020 after 2024's $222; only 9.9% of releases brought in $50,000 or more; the top 5% averaged over $300k — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/analysts-report-drop-in-game-revenue-on-steam-despite-growing-number-of-releases)

- A different methodology gives a much higher median: for titles released in 2026 the median revenue is $1,728, described as more than ten times lower than in 2016 and declining for 12 consecutive years — [Game World Observer, 2026-09-01](https://gameworldobserver.com/2026/09/01/the-median-revenue-of-games-on-steam-has-been-declining-for-12-consecutive-years)

- One secondary source states that of roughly 20,000 games released in 2025 only about 300 earned more than $1M (not confirmed against a primary dataset) — [Player.One](https://www.player.one/nearly-6000-games-steam-earned-100k-2025-valve-reveals-162428)
- Gamalytic's figures are estimates with a soft methodology; some coverage cites a possible error of up to 50% — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/over-5-000-games-released-on-steam-this-year-didnt-make-enough-money-to-recover-the-usd100-fee-to-put-a-game-on-valves-store-research-estimates)

**Market size and where the money goes**
- Third-party estimate: Steam generated about $16B in game revenue in 2025 (one secondary site cites $16.2B for Jan-Nov 2025, +50% vs. full-year 2024; treat growth figure with caution) — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/steam-earned-usd16b-in-2025-but-nearly-half-of-19-000-games-got-under-10-reviews); [Game Dev Report](https://gamedevreport.beehiiv.com/p/19-606-steam-games-made-16b-in-2025-most-made-0-but-that-s-not-a-problem)
- Alinea Analytics: Steam grossed an estimated $11.1B in H1 2026 (a half-year record), but titles launched in 2026 accounted for 21% of revenue, down from 27% in 2025 and 29% in 2024 — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/steam-hits-record-111-billion-as-older-games-drive-growth); [Alinea Analytics](https://alineaanalytics.substack.com/p/steam-is-having-another-record-year)
- Alinea Analytics: indie games generated about 25% of total Steam revenue by the end of 2025 — [Game World Observer, 2025-12-22](https://gameworldobserver.com/2025/12/22/indie-projects-generated-a-quarter-of-the-total-game-revenue-on-steam-by-the-end-of-2025-analytics)
- Steam Replay 2025 (Valve): only 14% of all user playtime in 2025 went to games released in 2025; 44% went to games 1-7 years old and 40% to games 8+ years old — [GamingOnLinux](https://gamingonlinux.com/2025/12/steam-replay-is-live-and-notes-only-14-of-playtime-spent-by-all-steam-users-was-for-2025-releases)
- Valve (GDC 2026): Steam passed 42M peak concurrent users, with 13.9M simultaneously in-game — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/steam-growth-continues-as-5863-games-cross-100k-revenue-in-2025)
- Valve (Gamescom, Aug 2025): from 2024-08-01 to 2025-08-01 Steam averaged 109,000 first-time buyers per day — [Game World Observer, 2025-08-29](https://gameworldobserver.com/2025/08/29/every-day-more-than-100000-new-buyers-appear-on-steam-and-other-figures-from-valves-report-at-gamescom-2025)

### Inferences
- The "median" depends heavily on what is counted: $249-$347 when every release (including hobby/asset-flip/AI-slop titles) is included, about $1,000-$1,700 under other methodologies. For a developer who actually ships a competent, marketed game, the relevant reference class is closer to "games that reach 10+ reviews" (roughly half of releases), not the whole pool.
- Rough percentile ladder for 2025 releases, combining the estimates above: about 60% above $100, about 34% above $1k, about 23% above $5k, about 10% above $50k, about 8% above $100k (all-time basis), and on the order of 1.5% above $1M (single unverified source), with about 3% reaching 1,000+ reviews.
- The audience and total spend keep growing (record concurrents, 109k new buyers/day, record number of $100k+ games), but new releases get a shrinking slice (21% of H1 2026 revenue; 14% of 2025 playtime). A first game competes mostly against the back catalogue and discounts, not just same-week launches.
- The 2026 surge in release count (already above full-year 2025 by mid-October) appears to be driven substantially by low-effort/AI-assisted releases; this inflates supply counts without necessarily raising competition at the quality tier where a polished game would sit.

### Gaps
- No verified mean (average) first-year revenue for indie games in 2025 was found; means are dominated by outliers and trackers rarely publish them. The "top 5% average over $300k" figure is the closest.
- Exact shares of 2025 releases above $10k and above $1M from a single transparent dataset were not found (only $5k, $50k, $100k cut-offs and one unverified $1M count).
- GameDiscoverCo's own median and tier breakdown for 2025 new releases appears to be paywalled / was not retrievable.
- A claim that the top 1% of Steam games earn 84.5% of estimated revenue appeared only on an unreliable-looking site and is not included as a finding.
- Final full-year 2026 release count is not yet available (year in progress).

## 2. Steam costs and terms (fees, revenue share, payout, refunds, pricing, tax)

### Takeaway
Entry cost is trivial ($100 per game, recoupable), and the standard cut is 30% of net revenue after VAT/sales tax and refunds; the 25%/20% tiers only start at $10M and $50M lifetime revenue and are irrelevant to a first game. The real "costs" for a Korea-based developer are US withholding tax on US-source sales (Korea is listed at 10% in Valve's tax FAQ), refunds, regional price discounts and currency conversion.

### Cited Findings
- Steam Direct fee: $100 per product (per app, not per developer); demos, soundtracks and DLC do not need their own fee — [Steamworks: Steam Direct](https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect); [Steamworks app fee doc](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/appfee?l=tchinese)
- The $100 fee is not refundable as cash but is recoupable: it is returned once the game has earned $1,000 in adjusted gross revenue — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-direct-fee-explained); [Fungies guide, 2026](https://fungies.io/steam-revenue-share-explained/)
- Revenue share: 30% up to $10M, 25% from $10M to $50M, 20% above $50M, measured on lifetime revenue per game and applied marginally (like tax brackets) — [Fungies guide, 2026](https://fungies.io/steam-revenue-share-explained/); [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/how-much-does-steam-take)
- The split is applied to adjusted gross revenue: what players paid minus VAT/sales taxes, refunds and chargebacks, not to the sticker price — [Fungies guide, 2026](https://fungies.io/steam-revenue-share-explained/)
- At a typical $10 price a developer nets roughly $7 per sale after Steam's cut (before tax and refunds) — [Spilled.gg](https://spilled.gg/5000-steam-games-2025-failed-make-back-100-submission-fee)
- GameDiscoverCo published an analysis titled "the numbers behind Steam's '24% cut' in 2025", indicating the platform-wide effective cut is below 30% once large games' lower tiers are included (only the title/abstract was accessible) — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/revealed-the-numbers-behind-steams)
- Tax: Valve's tax FAQ states withholding on US-source income can range from 0% to 30% depending on the tax interview/treaty; its country table lists Korea at 10% (column context not fully visible in the snippet; confirm in the Steamworks tax interview) — [Steamworks Tax FAQ](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/finance/taxfaq)
- Refund policy: full refunds are generally granted if requested within 14 days of purchase with under 2 hours of playtime; exceptions are sometimes granted beyond those limits — [Escapist (Korean)](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/kr/steam-refund-policy-guide-exceptions/)
- Since 2024, playtime during "Advanced Access"/pre-release early play counts toward the 2-hour refund limit — [Tom's Guide](https://tomsguide.com/gaming/steam-has-changed-its-policy-for-issuing-refunds-heres-what-you-should-know-before-playing-an-early-access-game); [Gamers Unchained](https://www.gamersunchained.com/news/steams-refund-policy-now-counts-advanced-access-as-part-of-the-time-played)

### Inferences
- For a first game the effective take-home is roughly: sticker price → minus VAT/GST in the buyer's country → minus refunds → minus 30% → minus US withholding on the US-source portion → then Korean income/corporate tax. A common planning figure is 50-60% of gross sticker revenue reaching the developer before home-country tax; this is an inference, not a sourced figure.
- The 2-hour refund window is a structural risk for very short games (under about 2 hours): players can finish and refund. Short, polished games need replayability or a price low enough that refunding is not worth the effort.
- Because the withholding (10% for Korea per Valve's table) applies only to US-source income, its overall drag is about 10% of the US share of sales (US is typically the largest or second-largest market), i.e., low single digits of total revenue.

### Gaps
- Payout threshold and schedule were not verified in this session. Background knowledge (unverified here): Valve pays monthly, about 30 days after month end, once the account's accrued balance reaches $100. Check the Steamworks payments FAQ.
- No published average refund rate for indie games was found; developer anecdotes commonly cite single-digit to low-teens percent, but nothing citable.
- No 2025-2026 source was retrieved on Valve's regional pricing recommendations (including KRW tiers). Background knowledge (unverified here): Valve suggests per-currency prices from the USD base price, updated its recommendation tables in late 2022, and developers can override per region; low-income regions get substantially lower suggested prices, which lowers average revenue per unit versus the USD price.
- Whether any change to the 30/25/20 tiers happened in 2025-2026 was not specifically checked; guides dated 2026 still describe the same tiers.

## 3. Discovery mechanics in 2025-2026 (wishlists, Next Fest, demos, Discovery Queue, Popular Upcoming, reviews)

### Takeaway
Steam discovery is momentum-amplifying: wishlists, Next Fest and the post-launch Discovery Queue all reward games that already show traction, and a wishlist is now worth about half what it was in 2020-2022 (about 0.22 first-month sales per wishlist vs. 0.45; median first-week conversion about 0.10-0.15x). Commonly cited targets are about 7,000-10,000 wishlists for Popular Upcoming (a threshold that may have changed in mid-2026) and 10+ reviews quickly after launch so that a review score displays.

### Cited Findings
**Wishlists and conversion**
- GameDiscoverCo (Steam games launched Sept 2024-Sept 2025, more than 25,000 wishlists at launch): median first-week sales were 0.15x launch wishlists (about 15,000 first-week sales per 100,000 wishlists); the count includes all first-week buyers, not only wishlisters — [GameDevReports recap](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-the-state-of-steam); [gam3s.gg](https://gam3s.gg/news/what-the-data-says-about-wishlist-conversion)
- Same study: titles priced above $10 at launch had a lower median of 0.10x — [GameDevReports weekly recap, Nov 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/weekly-gaming-reports-recap-november-681)
- Same study: games that under-performed on conversion had a median first-week user review score near 67%, versus about 91% for games that beat expectations — [gam3s.gg](https://gam3s.gg/news/what-the-data-says-about-wishlist-conversion)
- Zukowski: first-month sales per wishlist fell to about 0.22 for 2023-2025 releases from 0.45 for 2020-2022 ("wishlist inflation"), attributed to hover-tooltip wishlisting and the proliferation of Next Fests; predictive power of wishlists is described as unchanged — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/27273/steam-wishlist-inflation-value-of-1-wishlist-cut-in-half-but-predictive-power-remains-unchanged)
- An older (April 2024) benchmark cites medians of 22% first-week and 27% first-month wishlist conversion; attribution is disputed between GameDiscoverCo and Gamalytic — [GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-conversion-benchmarks); [ziva.sh](https://ziva.sh/tools/wishlist-to-sales-calculator)
- Launch-wishlist tiers attributed to Zukowski's benchmarks (secondary source, "updated June 2026"): Bronze 5,000, Silver 8,000, Gold 50,000, Diamond 90,000 — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-many-wishlists-before-launch)
- Actual outcomes vary 10-20x around any wishlist-based model — [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/how-many-wishlists-to-launch)

**Popular Upcoming**
- Historically the Popular Upcoming list was a chronological list of soon-to-launch games above a wishlist threshold of roughly 7,000-10,000; Steamworks' visibility documentation is cited as saying wishlists are not an algorithmic factor except for Popular Upcoming — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-algorithm-explained); [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/how-many-wishlists-to-launch)
- Unverified 2026 change: some secondary sources say Valve changed Popular Upcoming in June 2026 to algorithmic ranking and that the effective threshold rose from about 7,000 to at least 80,000 wishlists; a Steam store redesign in 2026 is also reported with concern that it disadvantages indies. None of this was confirmed against Valve — [dev.to](https://dev.to/linou518/steams-algorithm-isnt-magic-a-complete-breakdown-for-indie-games-in-2026-4hh4); [tech-insider.org](https://tech-insider.org/steam-store-redesign-2026/)

**Steam Next Fest and demos**
- Zukowski's February 2026 Next Fest survey (182 responses): tiers by wishlists earned during the fest run from Bronze (0-999) up to Diamond (10,000+); the surveyed median was 806 wishlists gained — [Zukowski benchmarks](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/); [ziva.sh](https://ziva.sh/blogs/steam-next-fest-2026)
- Same survey: games that launched their demo months before Next Fest earned roughly 2.5x the median wishlists of games that launched the demo during the fest — [ziva.sh](https://ziva.sh/blogs/steam-next-fest-2026)
- Same survey: Spearman correlation of 0.825 between pre-fest wishlists and wishlists earned during the fest; the fest mostly amplifies existing momentum — [ziva.sh](https://ziva.sh/blogs/steam-next-fest-2026)
- June 2026 Next Fest had over 4,300 demos, a record — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/who-won-june-2026s-steam-next-fest)

**Reviews, launch visibility, Discovery Queue**
- Steam does not display a review score until a game has 10 reviews; developers widely treat 10 reviews as a visibility trigger, though at least one analysis argues the "10 reviews flips the algorithm" belief is overstated — [Zukowski, 2022 (older than 2024)](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/01/25/why-your-first-10-reviews-are-the-most-important/); [firstlook.gg](https://firstlook.gg/blog/how-many-steam-reviews-does-your-game-need-at-launch)
- Valve staff have reportedly said the algorithm does not penalise "Mixed" review scores; the first 10 reviews can be of any quality — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-19806)
- After launch, games are placed in the Discovery Queue and tested on how many sales they generate; the initial burst of queue traffic typically fades after a few days if the game does not convert — [Zukowski, "What is the post-launch discovery queue", 2025-02-19](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/02/19/what-is-the-post-launch-discovery-queue/); [playio blog](https://blog.playio.co/steam-tags-discovery-queue-visibility)
- Steam Daily Deals featured 1,500+ titles in 2025; about 8.2M customers bought at least one Daily Deal (+125% YoY; another outlet says 8.6M) — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/steam-growth-continues-as-5863-games-cross-100k-revenue-in-2025); [Tech4Gamers](https://tech4gamers.com/5800-games-made-100k-on-steam-2025)

### Inferences
- Planning arithmetic with 2025 medians: 10,000 launch wishlists × 0.10-0.15 ≈ 1,000-1,500 first-week units; at a $15 price that is about $15k-$22k gross in week one, before the cut. Reaching a six-figure first year therefore typically needs tens of thousands of wishlists or a strong post-launch tail. (GameDiscoverCo's benchmark is for 25k+ wishlist games; conversion for smaller games is noisier.)
- Given the 0.825 correlation, Next Fest should be used late, as an amplifier, after the game already has a public demo, capsule and several thousand wishlists; a median participant gains only about 800 wishlists.
- First-week review score is a major conversion lever (67% vs. 91%); a polished, bug-free launch build matters more for conversion than additional pre-launch wishlists.
- The possible mid-2026 Popular Upcoming change, if real, removes one of the few "free" visibility slots that small games used to reach with about 7k wishlists; plans should not depend on it until verified.

### Gaps
- Valve's official description of launch "visibility rounds" (Update Visibility Rounds, launch visibility guarantee) was not retrieved in this session.
- The exact current Popular Upcoming rule (and whether the June 2026 change is real) is unconfirmed.
- June and October 2026 Next Fest survey medians were not found.
- No fresh (2025-2026) data on demo-to-wishlist conversion or on Valve's demo feature changes was retrieved. Background knowledge (unverified here): since mid-2024 demos can have their own store page and reviews and can appear on charts; a game may participate in only one Next Fest.
- The Zukowski launch tier table (5k/8k/50k/90k) comes from a secondary site and looks odd in its spacing; verify against howtomarketagame.com.

## 4. Genres and price points: what over-performs vs. what is oversaturated for small teams

### Takeaway
By hit counts, Steam in 2025 rewarded narrative, simulation, horror, RPG and idle/incremental games, plus co-op "friendslop", while 2D platformers, puzzle, arcade and racing sit at the bottom on median revenue with heavy oversupply. The best-selling new games cluster around a $15-$20 price, and the median price of top sellers has been falling ($19.50 in Feb 2023 to $15.64 in Oct 2025).

### Cited Findings
**Over-performing genres**
- Zukowski's 2025 analysis (1,000+ review "hits" by genre): Narrative 51, Simulation 43, Horror 39, RPG 28, Idle/Incremental 27; he notes the top-10 genre list has been remarkably stable over four years. The summary flags that the genre table has some internal inconsistencies — [Zukowski, 2026-01-27](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/01/27/what-the-hell-happened-in-2025/); summary: [Antlion Audio](https://antlionaudio.com/blogs/news/analysis-of-pc-gaming-in-2025-on-steam)
- By hit rate rather than raw count, open-world survival craft led in 2025: 15 of 72 releases (20.8%) reached 1,000 reviews (a genre generally beyond a solo first-timer's scope) — [Antlion Audio summary of Zukowski](https://antlionaudio.com/blogs/news/analysis-of-pc-gaming-in-2025-on-steam)
- Idle/incremental games are a growing share of releases, and Zukowski notes the revenue floor for a well-made idle game is quite high — [Zukowski Q1 2026 review, 2026-05-14](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/05/14/2026-q1-games/)
- Horror: Zukowski said in 2026 "Horror is still hot", but Q1 2026 had fewer horror hits than Q1 2025 (3 vs. 7; partial-year count) — [Zukowski Q1 2026 review](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/05/14/2026-q1-games/)
- Friendslop (cheap, clumsy co-op games): four of the ten best-selling Steam games of 2025 were friendslop titles (AppMagic) — [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/friendslop-steam-games-2025)
- Zukowski (2026-07-30): the number of friendslop games keeps growing but so does the audience; even at peak no single game captured more than about 10% of the friendslop audience, so he judges the niche not yet saturated — [Zukowski](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/07/30/is-friendslop-saturated/); [Game World Observer, 2026-08-03](https://gameworldobserver.com/2026/08/03/analyst-there-are-many-games-in-the-friendslap-niche-but-it-is-not-oversaturated)
- A study of about 100,000 Steam titles found sandbox games stand out on revenue; visual novels, simulation, RPG, horror and point-and-click have the next-highest medians — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/study-of-best-selling-genres-on-steam-shows-sandbox-games-continue-to-stand-out-in-revenue/)
- GameDiscoverCo 2025 revenue share by sub-genre: Base Building 7.22% of Steam revenue vs. Visual Novel 3.18%, Puzzle Platformer 3.09%, Puzzle 2.80% — [GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-steam-revenue-distribution)

**Oversaturated / under-performing genres**
- The lowest median earnings are in racing, puzzle, arcade and platformer games (around $1,000 or less) — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-revenue-by-genre)
- Platformer-related genres sit at the low end of revenue overall despite including major releases — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/study-of-best-selling-genres-on-steam-shows-sandbox-games-continue-to-stand-out-in-revenue/)
- 2D Platformer-tagged games are 9.5% of all Steam releases in 2026 but only 2.2% of the "millionaire" (over $1M) list (phrasing in the summary was ambiguous; verify) — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/other/item-52026)
- Supply examples for 2025: 1,458 games tagged Visual Novel and 702 tagged Precision Platformer were released — [Steam Page Analyzer niche pages](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/niches/visual-novel)

**Price points**
- GameDiscoverCo: the median price of each month's top-50 best-selling new Steam games (by units) fell from $19.50 in February 2023 to $15.64 in October 2025, a 20% drop — [GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-people-are-spending)
- AppMagic (via NextBigGames, Dec 2025) calls $19.99 "the indie default" and the most reliable price point; $9.99 is the most common indie price — [NextBigGames](https://nextbiggames.com/2025/12/29/steam-pricing-strategy-2025-from-0-to-69-99); [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-pricing-strategy)
- Newzoo (2026): $30 and below is the sweet spot for PC game prices — [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/110473/30-and-below-is-the-sweet-spot-for-pc-game-prices-analyst-firm-newzoo-finds/index.html)
- VG Insights argues indie developers charge too little, while games priced above $50 earn less (undated; likely older than 2024) — [VG Insights](https://wp.vginsights.com/?p=40)
- Wishlist conversion was lower for games priced above $10 (0.10x vs. 0.15x median) — [GameDevReports weekly recap, Nov 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/weekly-gaming-reports-recap-november-681)

### Inferences
- Visual novels are a split case: mid-table on median revenue (per the 100k-title study) but with heavy supply (1,458 in 2025) and a small share of total revenue (3.18%) and of big hits. They are "safe-ish but capped", not a growth genre.
- For a team whose edge is art and animation, the genres where visual identity is a recognised selling point and that also show up in the hit lists are narrative/adventure, horror (atmosphere), cozy/simulation and stylised roguelikes/deckbuilders; genres currently winning on "fun first, rough visuals" (friendslop, idle, survivors-likes) reward speed and concept more than polish.
- The 2025 breakout examples at low prices (PEAK around $8, Megabonk around $10, Schedule I around $20; prices are background knowledge) suggest that volume at $8-$20 beats premium pricing for unknown studios; the falling median price of top sellers supports this.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve Zukowski's full 2025 genre table (releases, hit counts and hit rates for platformers, puzzle, visual novels, roguelike deckbuilders, cozy, survivors-likes), so supply-adjusted hit rates for most genres are missing. Roguelike deckbuilders specifically: no 2025-2026 hit-rate figure found.
- No dataset was found comparing median revenue across $10/$15/$20 price points on a like-for-like basis.
- Match-3 on Steam: no specific data found.
- GameDiscoverCo's genre series ("Which genres have ruled Steam", "How have the genres of hit PC games changed") was located but not readable in this session.

## 5. How much visual polish / art quality matters

### Takeaway
Evidence is mixed and mostly anecdotal: capsule art measurably affects click-through and on-page wishlist conversion in individual cases, and bad capsules cost streamer coverage, but Zukowski's larger survey reportedly found CTR barely correlates with wishlist success, and the biggest 2025 breakouts were "rough but fun" games. Polish appears to be a hygiene factor and a multiplier for an appealing concept in the right genre, not an independent driver of sales.

### Cited Findings
- Zukowski case comparison (2020; older than 2024): two games with similar impressions had capsule click-through rates of 9.6% (Lofi Ping Pong) vs. 6.7% (Omen Exitio), and page-visit-to-wishlist rates of 4.7% vs. about 2.7% — [Zukowski, 2020-11-23](https://howtomarketagame.com/2020/11/23/why-your-steam-page-matters/)
- Zukowski (2022; older than 2024): analysis of Clanfolk and of streamer SplatterCat's coverage argues capsule art affects whether YouTubers/streamers pick up a game; a poor capsule can be the main obstacle — [Game World Observer, 2022-07-18](https://gameworldobserver.com/2022/07/18/why-eye-catching-capsule-art-is-essential-for-games-marketing-and-its-youtube-coverage); [Zukowski, 2022-07-18](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/07/18/how-do-you-get-streamers-to-cover-your-game-get-a-better-capsule/)
- Secondhand claim: Zukowski surveyed over 100 games and found CTR barely correlates with wishlist success; impression volume was the metric that tracked outcomes (not verified against his original post) — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-ctr-benchmarks)
- Disputed: one guide says Steam's algorithm heavily favours high-CTR games; another argues a low CTR does not make Steam show a game less, it just yields fewer visits and wishlists — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-optimization-guide); [playio blog](https://blog.playio.co/steam-capsule-art-wishlist-conversion)
- Third-party heuristics for capsule CTR: 2-4% is average, about 2% poor, 7%+ excellent — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-ctr-benchmarks)
- Zukowski (as reported by GamesRadar) describes a PC "golden age of rough but fun games that devs can make quickly", citing PEAK and Megabonk: Steam players "want fun first" — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/steam-expert-hails-pc-gaming-golden-age-of-rough-but-fun-games-that-devs-can-make-quickly-and-players-love-from-peak-to-megabonk-steam-players-want-fun-first)
- First-week review score separates conversion over- and under-performers (about 91% vs. 67% positive) — [gam3s.gg](https://gam3s.gg/news/what-the-data-says-about-wishlist-conversion)

### Inferences
- On Steam, the capsule and first screenshots/trailer seconds act as a genre-and-quality signal: strong art raises click-through and wishlist rate per impression, but total impressions (driven by genre demand, festivals, streamers, algorithmic momentum) dominate outcomes. High art quality in a low-demand genre (e.g., a beautiful 2D platformer) is not rescued by the art.
- High-quality art and animation is most monetisable when it makes the game instantly legible and "streamable" in a genre with proven demand; it also raises costs and development time, which works against the 2025-2026 pattern of fast, cheap, concept-led hits.
- Polish affects review score (bugs, feel, UX), and review score strongly affects conversion and tail, so execution quality matters even where raw art fidelity does not.

### Gaps
- No rigorous 2024-2026 study correlating art style (pixel vs. hand-drawn 2D vs. low-poly vs. realistic 3D) with Steam sales or wishlists was found. This is a genuine evidence gap; claims in this area are mostly opinion.
- No public Valve data on capsule A/B testing outcomes was found.
- Zukowski's ">100 games CTR survey" could not be located at the primary source.

## 6. Recent solo / small-team successes and typical failures

### Takeaway
The headline 2025 small-team hits (Schedule I, PEAK, Megabonk) were cheap, mechanically "sticky", streamer-friendly games made by one person or a tiny team, not high-fidelity art showcases; they are extreme outliers against a base rate where about half of releases never reach 10 reviews and 40% gross under $100.

### Cited Findings
- Schedule I (TVGS, 2025, Early Access): built solo by Tyler Hankinson (aged 22 at release) using Unity and Blender; revenue estimates range from about $125M by May 2025 to $130M-$151M (Game Dev Report / Alinea Analytics); described as Steam's most popular indie game of 2025 — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/walter-white-simulator-schedule-i-becomes-steam-s-most-popular-indie-game-of-2025); [Game Dev Report](https://gamedevreport.beehiiv.com/p/19-606-steam-games-made-16b-in-2025-most-made-0-but-that-s-not-a-problem)
- PEAK (2025, small team): 11M+ copies on Steam and about $83M estimated revenue — [Game Dev Report](https://gamedevreport.beehiiv.com/p/19-606-steam-games-made-16b-in-2025-most-made-0-but-that-s-not-a-problem)
- Megabonk (solo developer "vedinad", released 2025-09-18): 1 million copies in two weeks; later revenue estimate about $27M (an earlier estimate at 1M copies was about $10M gross) — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/roguelike/out-of-nowhere-roguelike-hit-megabonk-has-sold-1-million-copies-in-2-weeks-solo-creator-says-ill-be-eating-spaghetti-with-extra-sauce-tonight); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Vampire-Survivors-esque-indie-reverse-bullet-hell-game-Megabonk-sells-1-million-copies-in-just-two-weeks.1132354.0.html)
- One Last Hand (2026, six-person team, card battler): reportedly about 12,000 net copies in two weeks, with roughly 90% of sales from China (self-reported on Reddit, unverified) — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-indie-One-Last-Hand-reportedly-sells-12-000-copies-in-two-weeks-with-roughly-90-coming-from-China.1397287.0.html)
- The King Is Watching (tinyBuild-published, 2025): reached 200k and then 500k sales, with Steam feature changes and a China strategy/localisation cited as pivotal — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-the-king-is-watching-sold-200k)
- Small Spaces (indie, covered by GameDiscoverCo for selling about 15x its week-one sales): country split 25% US, 13% China, 10% Germany, 6% UK, 4% France, 4% Canada, 3% Brazil — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-small-spaces-sold-15x-its-week)
- Indie titles ranked among Steam's top revenue games of 2025 (e.g., Schedule I and Hollow Knight: Silksong in the top 12) — [Technobezz](https://www.technobezz.com/news/hollow-knight-silksong-and-schedule-i-rank-among-steams-top-2025-12-30-e07g)
- Typical failure base rate: nearly half of 2025 releases under 10 reviews; 40% under $100 gross; 65.9% under $1,000 (Gamalytic, partial-year 2025) — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/more-than-19-000-games-launched-on-steam-this-year-but-almost-half-have-fewer-than-10-reviews); [Spilled.gg](https://spilled.gg/5000-steam-games-2025-failed-make-back-100-submission-fee)

### Inferences
- The 2025 breakouts share traits: co-op or highly watchable loops, low price, simple or deliberately rough visuals, fast iteration, and virality through streamers/TikTok rather than paid marketing. None is evidence that high art budgets drive first-game success; Silksong (a sequel with a huge existing audience) is the art-led counterexample and is not a first-time case.
- Long-tail cases (Small Spaces at about 15x week-one sales; The King Is Watching going from 200k to 500k) show that for well-reviewed games, lifetime revenue can be several multiples of launch-week revenue, which matters more to small teams than launch spikes.

### Gaps
- Budgets and development times for these hits were not found in this session. Background knowledge (unverified here): PEAK was made by Aggro Crab and Landfall in roughly a month-long game jam held in South Korea in early 2025 and launched at about $8; Schedule I was reportedly in development for several years by its solo developer.
- R.E.P.O., Balatro (2024), Blue Prince, Clair Obscur and other 2024-2026 small-team hits were not researched for budget/time data.
- No first-hand postmortems of typical failed first games (with wishlists, spend and revenue) were retrieved; failure evidence here is statistical only.
- No Korea-based solo/first-time Steam success from 2024-2026 with verified figures was researched.

## 7. Steam user geography and localisation impact

### Takeaway
Steam's audience is roughly split between English and Simplified Chinese speakers (about a third each per Valve's own figures), with China described as Steam's largest market by users/owners; monthly hardware-survey language shares swing wildly and are unreliable. Simplified Chinese localisation is the highest-leverage language investment and can account for anywhere from about 13% to over half of an individual game's sales.

### Cited Findings
- Valve data (as summarised from Valve's "Steam in 2025" materials): most-used languages on Steam are Simplified Chinese 33.6%, English 33%, Russian 8.4% — [GameDevReports: Valve, Steam in 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/valve-steam-in-2025)
- Valve: the fastest-growing markets by new users from 2020 to 2025 were Japan, Brazil, China, Germany and the USA — [Game World Observer, 2025-08-29](https://gameworldobserver.com/2025/08/29/every-day-more-than-100000-new-buyers-appear-on-steam-and-other-figures-from-valves-report-at-gamescom-2025)
- Alinea Analytics (2025 year in review): China is the largest market on Steam; China's share of audience was 64% for Escape From Duckov, 44% for Split Fiction and 26% for Monster Hunter Wilds — [GameDevReports: Alinea 2025 Year in Review](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/alinea-analytics-2025-year-in-review)
- Stellar Blade (Korean-developed, Steam release 2025): over half (about 56% in one analysis) of its roughly 1.5M Steam copies came from China — [games.gg](https://games.gg/ja/news/china-role-in-steam-best-sellers/)
- Steam hardware survey language shares in 2026 are volatile: Simplified Chinese 54.60% in February 2026 (+30.74 points in one month, English 22.27%), back to about 23% in March, 31.39% in September, 21.98% in October (English 39.04%). Analysts attribute the swings to sampling; Valve does not weight by region, and language setting is not nationality — [GamingOnLinux, Mar 2026](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/03/steam-survey-for-february-2026-shows-a-big-swing-to-simplified-chinese); [GamingOnLinux, Oct 2026](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/10/steam-survey-for-september-2026-shows-a-drop-in-linux-with-simplified-chinese-rising/); [Gigazine, Apr 2026](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260403-steam-hardware-software)
- Steam is reported to hold about 87% of China's PC game store market — [Game Industry Library: China's Steam PC gaming market in 2025](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/china-s-steam-pc-gaming-market-in-2025/read)
- Typical Western-leaning indie split (Small Spaces): US 25%, China 13%, Germany 10%, UK 6%, France 4%, Canada 4%, Brazil 3% — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-small-spaces-sold-15x-its-week)
- Localisation before/after case (Defender's Quest, around 2016-2017; older than 2024): 45% of the game's lifetime Steam revenue from China was earned in the single week after adding Chinese localisation — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/china-is-kind-of-a-big-deal-for-indie-games)
- Analysts partly attribute Steam's 2025 revenue growth to rising player numbers from Asia, especially China — [PC Games Hardware (German)](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Steam-Software-69900/News/11-Milliarden-Dollar-Umsatz-im-ersten-Halbjahr-1547987/)

### Inferences
- A sensible minimum localisation set for a Korea-based developer targeting revenue is English + Simplified Chinese + Korean (native) + Japanese, with Russian, German, Brazilian Portuguese, French and Spanish as later additions; this ordering follows the language-share and growth-market data above but is an inference, not a sourced ranking.
- China's revenue share is lower than its user share because of regional pricing (lower CNY prices), so unit share overstates revenue share.
- Text-light or text-efficient designs make broad localisation cheap; narrative-heavy games (a genre with many hits) carry much higher localisation costs, which matters for a small budget.
- Genre and theme fit to Chinese players (card battlers, roguelikes, co-op, extraction) can produce extreme skews (64-90% China), a potential advantage for an East Asia-based developer.

### Gaps
- No Valve or third-party 2025-2026 revenue share by country was found (only users/languages/owners). Korea's share of Steam users or revenue was not found at all.
- No controlled 2024-2026 study quantifying the sales lift from Simplified Chinese, Japanese or Korean localisation was found; evidence is case-level.
- Whether releasing in mainland China via Steam Global faces any new regulatory risk in 2026 was not researched.

## 8. Steam Deck / SteamOS / new Valve hardware, and AI-content disclosure rules

### Takeaway
Valve is extending the Deck "Verified" model to a living-room Steam Machine and the Steam Frame VR headset (announced November 2025, targeted for 2026), so controller support and modest performance targets are cheap ways to qualify for more surfaces. AI rules were relaxed in January 2026: only AI-generated content that players actually consume must be disclosed; behind-the-scenes AI tools are exempt.

### Cited Findings
**Hardware**
- Valve announced three devices in November 2025: Steam Machine (console-like PC), Steam Frame (VR headset) and a new Steam Controller, initially for early 2026 — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/hardware/valve-announces-steam-machine-frame-controller); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/valve-announces-new-steam-machine-steam-frame-vr-headset-and-controller/)
- Valve later narrowed the window to summer 2026 (a slip from "early 2026") with no price announced at that time; outlet price estimates of $699-$999 for the Steam Machine are speculation — [VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/steam-machine-and-steam-frame-confirmed-for-summer-2026-by-valve/); [Engadget](https://engadget.com/2187938/steam-machine-and-steam-frame-are-coming-this-summer)
- Verified programme expansion: Steam Machine Verified games are expected to support the same input methods as Steam Deck and run at a minimum of 1080p at 30 fps; requirements are described as nearly identical to Deck Verified, and a separate Steam Frame Verified programme exists — [Engadget](https://engadget.com/gaming/pc/valves-steam-machine-launches-in-2026-everything-we-know-so-far-200458597.html); [VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/steam-machine-and-steam-frame-confirmed-for-summer-2026-by-valve/)

**AI disclosure**
- Valve began allowing games with AI-generated content, subject to disclosure, in January 2024 — [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/95518/valve-will-now-allow-games-with-ai-generated-content-to-be-released-on-steam/index.html)
- Around January 16-17, 2026 Valve rewrote the Steamworks AI disclosure form: disclosure is required only for AI-generated content consumed by players (art, audio, text, marketing assets shipped to players); AI-powered efficiency tools used behind the scenes (e.g., code assistants) are exempt — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/steam-updates-ai-disclosure-form-to-specify-that-its-focused-on-ai-generated-content-that-is-consumed-by-players-not-efficiency-tools-used-behind-the-scenes/); [Gigazine, 2026-01-20](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260120-steam-updates-ai-disclosure-guidelines)
- The form distinguishes pre-generated AI content from live-generated AI content; live generation requires describing guardrails, and players can report illegal content produced by live-generated AI — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-updates-AI-disclosure-form-requiring-developers-to-report-visible-and-in-game-AI-but-not-background-tools.1206103.0.html); [GameMeca](https://www.gamemeca.com/en/view.php?gid=1770331)
- 2026's record release volume is being linked in press coverage to generative-AI and "vibe-coded" games — [Kotaku, 2026-10-16](https://kotaku.com/steam-valve-slop-ai-genai-vibe-coding-pc-game-releases-2000746749)

### Inferences
- For a small 2D or stylised game, Deck/Steam Machine Verified status is low-cost (full controller support, readable text at 1280x800, no launcher, 30 fps+) and is worth designing for from the start, since Valve gives Verified titles dedicated store surfaces.
- For an art-quality-focused developer, the AI disclosure rule cuts both ways: disclosed AI art appears on the store page and draws player backlash in art-sensitive communities, while hand-made art is a differentiator in a year when AI-assisted releases are flooding the store. Using AI coding assistance needs no disclosure under the January 2026 wording.

### Gaps
- Whether the Steam Machine and Steam Frame actually shipped in summer 2026, their final prices, and any sales figures were not confirmed in this session.
- No Valve figure for Steam Deck/SteamOS share of playtime or sales in 2025-2026 was found; the hardware survey's Linux share was mentioned only in headline form. Steam Deck's cumulative unit sales were not found from a reliable source.
- One outlet claims a further mandatory AI metadata change in June 2026; this conflicts with the January 2026 reporting and is unverified — [remio.ai](https://www.remio.ai/post/indie-game-studios-are-rejecting-steam-s-new-mandatory-ai-metadata-tags)
- Valve's own Steamworks documentation text on AI disclosure was not read directly.
