# Mobile game market (Google Play / Apple App Store) as a target platform for a first-time indie developer, 2025-2026

> Research date: 2026-11-18. Method caveat for the report writer: in this session full-page fetching (WebFetch) was blocked by the network allowlist, so every finding below comes from web-search result summaries of the linked pages, not from reading the full pages or PDFs. Numbers are reproduced as the search results reported them; where two sources disagree, both are given. Primary-report URLs are included so figures can be verified. Items I could not source in this session are listed under "Gaps", never under "Cited Findings".

## 1. Market size, iOS vs Android split, growth trend, and revenue concentration

### Takeaway
Mobile is the largest games platform (about $113B in 2025 and a forecast $121B in 2026 per Newzoo; about $82B of store IAP per Sensor Tower), but it is a mature, flat-to-shrinking market in store terms: IAP revenue grew about 1% in 2025 and fell 2% in H1 2026 while downloads are falling fast (-7% in 2025, -12% in H1 2026). iOS takes roughly two-thirds of store revenue with about 15% of downloads; Google Play takes about 81% of downloads with roughly a third of revenue, and the charts are dominated by old live-service titles and giant publishers.

### Cited Findings
- Sensor Tower State of Mobile 2026 (covers calendar 2025): mobile games IAP revenue was $81.8B, +1% YoY; non-game apps reached $85.6B (+21%) and overtook games for the first time. Headline figures combine iOS and Google Play worldwide, but China is iOS only (third-party Android stores excluded). — [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/state-of-mobile-2026); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/apps-beat-games-in-mobile-spend-for-the-first-time-in-a-decade-sensor-tower/)
- 2025 mobile game downloads fell 7% vs 2024 while revenue rose 1%. — [9to5Mac summary of Sensor Tower](https://9to5mac.com/?p=1036095)
- "Over 95,000 mobile games were downloaded every minute in 2025" (Sensor Tower data). — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/over-95000-mobile-games-were-downloaded-every-minute-in-2025)
- Platform revenue split, 2025: App Store game revenue $52.5B; Google Play roughly $30B (secondary summaries of Sensor Tower State of Gaming 2026). App Store IAP revenue was flat, Google Play posted modest gains, downloads declined on both. — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/02/25/app-store-gaming-revenue-hit-52-5b-in-2025-topping-google-play-and-steam-combined-report/); [Sensor Tower State of Gaming 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/state-of-gaming-2026); [report PDF mirror](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026-02-26-sensor_tower__state_of_gaming_2026__en_wp.pdf)
- Platform download split, 2025: Google Play 81% of all game downloads, App Store 15%. 96% of mobile game downloads were free-to-play titles (99% on Google Play). — [Sensor Tower State of Gaming 2026, via summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026); [Global Games Forum breakdown](https://www.globalgamesforum.com/news/state-of-gaming-heres-what-the-mobile-data-actually-says)
- H1 2026 (most recent): global mobile gaming IAP revenue fell 2% to $40B; new game downloads fell 12% to 24B. — [Outlook Respawn on Sensor Tower H1 2026](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/mobile-gaming-iap-revenue-drops-2-as-ad-spend-surges-in-2026); [tech-insider.org](https://tech-insider.org/mobile-game-revenue-decline-2026/)
- Newzoo Global Games Market Report 2026: mobile reached $113.3B in 2025 (+10.7% YoY) out of a $201.6B total market; forecast $121.1B in 2026 (+6.8%, about 57% of a $213.9B market); 3.10B mobile players in 2026 (+3.9%); mobile ARPPU $96.6 in 2026. Newzoo says mobile revenue growth is "increasingly decoupled from downloads" (deeper monetisation of established live-service titles offsets falling downloads). - Newzoo H1 2026: global mobile downloads fell 25% YoY while cost per install rose 30% to $0.56 (as reported by PocketGamer.biz; another search summary attributes the "+30% to $0.56" CPI figure to Adjust's gaming report). — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/no-single-model-for-growth-says-newzoo/)

- Global gaming app UA (advertising) spend reached $25B in 2025 (AppsFlyer). — [Mobile Marketing Reads](https://mobilemarketingreads.com/?p=49901)
- Concentration signals: Tencent alone earned $354.6M in December 2025, more than the 2nd and 3rd publishers combined. — [GamingonPhone, top 15 publishers Dec 2025](https://gamingonphone.com/news/top-15-mobile-game-publishers-for-december-2025)

- AppMagic casual report: the casual mobile market "remains heavily concentrated", with Puzzle and Casino dominating revenue; high revenue concentration limits entry for new titles. — [Mobidictum summary of AppMagic Casual Games Report 2025](https://mobidictum.com/appmagic-casual-games-report-2025-summary/)
- South Korea: Sensor Tower's Korea report says revenue per download for Korean mobile games hit an all-time high while Korean mobile game downloads shrank from about 540M to about 390M (years not stated in the summary). — [Sensor Tower Korea report (KR)](https://sensortower.com/ko/blog/state-of-gaming-in-korea-2026-report-KR); [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/28017/sensor-tower-analyzes-shifting-monetization-trends-in-korean-mobile-games)

### Inferences
- Derived (not directly reported): Sensor Tower's $81.8B at +1% implies about $81B store IAP in 2024; Newzoo's $113.3B at +10.7% implies about $102B in 2024. The gap between trackers (Sensor Tower about $82B, Newzoo about $113B, AppMagic about $57B) is definitional: Sensor Tower = gross iOS + Google Play IAP; Newzoo adds third-party Android stores (notably China) and uses its own consumer-spend model; AppMagic's figures are lower (likely net of store fees and a narrower scope). Reports should name the tracker with every number.
- 95,000 downloads/minute implies roughly 50B game downloads in 2025 (my arithmetic: 95,000 x 525,600 minutes).
- $52.5B / ($52.5B + about $30B) implies iOS is about 64% of store game revenue from about 15% of downloads; revenue per download on iOS is therefore on the order of 8-10x Google Play globally. For a quality-focused premium-feel game, iOS is the revenue platform; Android is the volume/ad platform.
- A market where revenue is flat, downloads are falling 7-25% a year and CPI is rising 30% is a "share-taking" market, structurally hostile to new entrants without capital.

### Gaps
- No reliable figure found for total in-app advertising revenue of mobile games for 2024/2025/2026 (the trackers above report IAP/consumer spend only). The split "IAP + ads" requested could not be completed.
- No direct statistic found for "share of revenue earned by the top 1% of publishers / top 100 games" for 2025-2026. Only the qualitative/indirect concentration signals above.
- No iOS vs Google Play (vs ONE store) revenue split specific to South Korea was found.
- Sensor Tower's own 2024 base figures were not retrieved; the 2024 values above are derived.

## 2. Number of games / releases, typical indie earnings, share of games that are profitable

### Takeaway
Roughly 200,000+ new mobile games shipped in 2025 (about 600 a day) into a market with falling downloads, and trackers estimate only about 10% of releases attract any meaningful user attention. I found no trustworthy median-revenue figure for indie mobile games; the best available proxies (83% of launched games dead within 3 years, very low median retention) point to a median outcome near zero.

### Cited Findings
- Sensor Tower State of Gaming 2026: in 2025 the App Store received 55,300 new game titles (+11.4% YoY) and Google Play over 150,000 (+45.9% YoY); a separate summary gives 225 thousand games released in total, about 617 per day. — [GameDev Reports summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026); [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/state-of-gaming-2026)
- Google Play reduced store inventory by about 47% (total app count) between the start of 2024 and early 2025 in a quality/policy crackdown, but game releases rebounded sharply in 2025. — [Sensor Tower State of Gaming 2026, via summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026)
- AppMagic Mobile Market Landscape 2026: more than 1.4M releases (apps and games combined) launched across Google Play and the App Store in 2025, 25% more than in 2024; the share of games rose from 63% (2024) to 72% (2025); only about 10% of releases attract user attention. (AppMagic's counting methodology clearly differs from Sensor Tower's; the two release counts are not reconcilable.) — [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-market-landscape); [AppMagic PDF](https://appmagic.rocks/files/view/upload/Reports/EN_MobileMarkeLandscape2026.pdf)
- SuperScale / Atomik Research survey of 504 mobile developers in the US and UK (published late 2023, i.e. older than 2024): 83% of launched mobile games die within three years; only 5% receive support beyond seven years; 76% of launched games hit peak revenue within year one. SuperScale sells growth services, so its framing is interested. — [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/100948-new-report-reveals-83-mobile-games-fail-first.html); [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/superscale-83-of-launched-mobile)
- Unity Gaming Report 2025: 75% of studios with fewer than 10 employees made most of their revenue from mobile games (2024 data). — [Unity Gaming Report](https://unity.com/resources/gaming-report?isGated=false); [Statista chart](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1609502/game-devs-most-money-from-mobile-by-size)
- PocketGamer.biz survey: 90% of developers' most recent games launched on mobile. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/90-of-devs-most-recent-games-launched-on-mobile)
- GameAnalytics (2025 data): the median mobile game retains about 22% of players on day 1, under 4% on day 7 and about 0.7% on day 30 (see section 3), i.e. the median game cannot sustain a paying audience. — [GameDev Reports on GameAnalytics 2026 benchmarks](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)

### Inferences
- With 150k-225k new games a year, falling downloads and paid installs now the majority (section 3), the default outcome for a first-time developer who simply uploads a game is near-zero downloads and near-zero revenue; this is consistent with, though not proven by, the "about 10% attract attention" and "83% dead within 3 years" figures.
- Mobile remains where small studios actually earn (Unity: 75% of sub-10-person studios), but those are survivors, and many of them are ad-monetized casual studios working with publishers, not premium/art-led developers.

### Gaps
- No credible, recent (2024-2026) source was found for median revenue of an indie mobile game, the share of games earning under $1,000, or the percentage of mobile games that are profitable. Analytics firms' samples (e.g. Appfigures' top 1,000) exclude the long tail by construction. This should be reported as unknown rather than estimated.
- Total number of live games on each store (as opposed to new releases per year) was not found for 2025-2026.

## 3. User-acquisition economics: CPI, ATT/SKAN, Privacy Sandbox, retention, LTV vs CPI, organic discovery

### Takeaway
Paid UA now drives the majority of installs (57% on Android, 44% on iOS in 2025) and costs are rising (blended CPI +30% YoY) while downloads fall; iOS installs cost several times Android installs, and median retention (D1 22% / D7 under 4% / D30 0.7%) is far below what is needed to recoup tier-1 CPIs. Organic-only discovery is not a realistic growth plan for a new developer in 2025-2026, though organic share is higher in Japan and for non-hypercasual genres.

### Cited Findings
- AppsFlyer State of Gaming App Marketing 2026 (2025 data, 24.8B installs of which 14.1B paid): paid share of installs rose about 10% YoY on both platforms; paid installs were 57% of total on Android and 44% on iOS. Hypercasual is most paid-dependent: 81% of installs on Android and 67% on iOS are paid. In Japan, Android midcore paid share was just 17% in Q4 2025 vs 48% in the US. — [GameDev Reports on AppsFlyer](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appsflyer-the-state-of-the-mobile); [AppsFlyer report page](https://www.appsflyer.com/resources/reports/gaming-app-marketing-report/)
- Adjust Gaming App Insights 2026: the global paid-to-organic install ratio rose 61%, from 2.07 to 3.33 (2025 vs 2024); average number of acquisition partners per app fell from 6 to 5.3. — [Barchart / Adjust press release](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/666607/adjust-gaming-app-sessions-rose-in-2025-as-retention-becomes-a-core-growth-strategy); [Adjust report PDF](https://a.storyblok.com/f/47007/x/20551740ee/gaming-app-insights-report-2026.pdf)
- Blended global mobile gaming CPI rose 30% YoY to $0.56 (H1 2026; attributed to Newzoo by PocketGamer.biz and to Adjust by another summary). — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/no-single-model-for-growth-says-newzoo/)
- Liftoff 2025 Casual Gaming Apps Report (data Feb 2024-Feb 2025): casual game CPI about $1.41 on iOS vs $0.14 on Android (as reported in the search summary; verify against the report). Simulation, action and racing games see roughly 10x higher CPIs on iOS than Android. Across genres, casino had the highest iOS CPI ($21.03) and RPG the highest Android CPI ($4.29). — [Liftoff highlights](https://liftoff.ai/?p=35032); [GameDev Reports on Liftoff & Singular](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/liftoff-and-singular-casual-games)
- Mid-core: overall CPI $2.03; iOS $3.86, Android $0.73 (Keewano article, underlying source unclear, 2024-2025 data). — [Keewano](https://keewano.com/blog/mobile-mid-core-games-trends/)
- Hyper-casual 2025 global median CPI about $0.40 on Android (attributed to Adjust by a vendor blog). — [Mapendo](https://mapendo.co/blog/mobile-games-cpi-2025)
- Platform-wide 2026 averages conflict between vendor blogs: tier-1 averages of about $4.22 (iOS) and $2.97 (Android) in one; global averages of $2.24 (iOS) and $1.12 (Android) in another. Treat as directional only. — [FoxData](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/2026-mobile-game-user-acquisition-cost-benchmarks-how-much-should-you-spend/); [The Social Outline](https://thesocialoutline.com/blog/mobile-app-cpi-benchmarks-2026)
- Retention, GameAnalytics 2026 benchmarks (calendar-2025 data, mobile games with 1,000+ MAU; sample cited as 16,262 games in one summary and 11,600 in another): median D1 about 22%, top quartile just over 30%, top decile about 40%; median D7 below 4% (3.4-3.9%), top 25% about 7-8%; median D30 about 0.7% (0.68-0.79%), top 10% about 4%. — [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game); [report PDF mirror](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-27-2026-mobile-pc-benchmarks_compressed.pdf); [Game Growth Advisor analysis](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-kpis-benchmarks-2026/)
- Caveat from the same analysis: pooled all-games medians should be treated as context, not targets, for commercially scaled games (marketed titles sit well above the pooled median). — [Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-kpis-benchmarks-2026/)
- ATT: Adjust reports gaming ATT opt-in (of users shown the prompt) of 37.9% in Q1 2025, 39% in Q1 2026; industry-wide 35% in Q2 2025; arcade (59.3%), sports (54.1%) and action (45.9%) highest. Most iOS users therefore remain untrackable at user level; SKAdNetwork / AdAttributionKit (iOS 17.4+, expanded at WWDC 2025 with configurable windows and re-engagement) handle the rest in aggregated form. — [Adjust ATT opt-in rates 2025](https://www.adjust.com/blog/att-opt-in-rates-2025); [Aarki on iOS attribution in 2026](https://www.aarki.com/insights/ios-attribution-in-2026-5-things-marketers-need-to-know/); [AdExchanger on post-ATT game UA](https://www.adexchanger.com/mobile/ua-for-game-developers-is-an-entirely-different-animal-in-the-post-att-era/)
- Google Privacy Sandbox: Google announced on 2025-10-17 that it was ending most Privacy Sandbox advertising technologies; the planned deprecation of the Android advertising ID (GAID) therefore did not happen, though sources disagree on how long GAID is guaranteed. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/chrome-kills-most-privacy-sandbox-technologies-after-adoption-fails/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-kills-privacy-sandbox-3608497); [Mobile Dev Memo on GAID](https://mobiledevmemo.com/with-googles-cookie-policy-reversal-whats-the-fate-of-android-gaid/)
- Organic uplift caution: extra "organic" installs that are actually caused by paid campaigns are easily mistaken for true organic performance. — [Playio blog](https://blog.playio.co/viral-coefficient-organic-uplift-mobile-games)

### Inferences
- LTV vs CPI (my arithmetic on cited benchmarks): with casual ARPDAU of $0.10-0.30 (section 4) and median retention, a median game yields only a few cents of lifetime revenue per install, far below even the blended $0.56 CPI, let alone $1.4-3.9 iOS CPIs. Only games at top-decile retention (D1 about 40%, D30 about 4%) with strong monetization can run paid UA profitably. No source gave a direct, recent LTV/CPI benchmark.
- Android measurement remains deterministic (GAID survived), iOS remains probabilistic/aggregated; this favors large advertisers with data science teams and makes small-budget iOS UA testing noisy.
- For a Korea-based solo developer, realistic non-paid channels are store featuring (editorial), short-form social/video virality, press/community, and cross-promotion from a PC/console release; pure ASO in a 600-games-a-day store is unlikely to suffice. Japan's low paid share suggests organic/brand discovery works better there than in the US.

### Gaps
- No primary, genre-by-platform CPI table for 2025-2026 from AppsFlyer/Liftoff/Adjust could be read in full; the casual Android $0.14 figure in particular looks low and should be verified.
- No Korea-specific CPI or retention benchmarks found.
- No sourced LTV benchmarks by genre; no data specifically on indie discovery without a UA budget (featuring odds, organic install volumes for unfeatured new games).

## 4. Monetization models and benchmarks (IAP, ads/eCPM, hybrid-casual, subscriptions, premium, ARPDAU)

### Takeaway
F2P is the market (96% of downloads), with revenue per daily user ranging from roughly $0.10-0.30 (casual/hypercasual, ad-led) to $2-10+ (hardcore RPG/strategy, IAP-led); hybrid monetization (ads + IAP) is the only growing casual model. Premium paid mobile is a small niche but is growing and demonstrably viable for a recognizable, award-winning game ported from PC/console (Balatro: about $21M mobile revenue in 2025), not as a mobile-first launch from an unknown.

### Cited Findings
- 96% of 2025 mobile game downloads were free-to-play (99% on Google Play). — [Sensor Tower State of Gaming 2026 via Global Games Forum](https://www.globalgamesforum.com/news/state-of-gaming-heres-what-the-mobile-data-actually-says)
- ARPDAU ranges (2026 vendor glossary, blended global, IAP + ads): casual/hyper-casual $0.10-0.30; mid-core $0.50-2; hardcore RPG/strategy/sim $2-10+. Revenue mix: hyper-casual 80%+ ads; RPG/strategy 80%+ IAP; casual puzzle more balanced. Low-confidence source (vendor glossary, not a dataset). — [MWM glossary](https://mwm.ai/glossary/arpdau); [Playio ARPDAU benchmarks](https://blog.playio.co/arpdau-benchmarks-mobile-games)
- The same analysis notes published ARPDAU benchmarks cluster around ad-driven genres and that there is no trustworthy public primary source for IAP-heavy midcore/RPG ARPDAU. — [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/arpdau-benchmarks-mobile-games)
- Appodeal 2025 report on about 10,000 US Android casual games: lifetime ad revenue per user about $0.86 for hypercasual, $4.90 for party games, $14.83 for merge-3 (per user, not per DAU). — [Playio citing Appodeal](https://blog.playio.co/arpdau-benchmarks-mobile-games)
- eCPM, Appodeal Q4 2024 (flag: late-2024 data): interstitial eCPM South Korea $10.04 iOS / $11.23 Android; United States $14.32 iOS / $14.08 Android. Rewarded video has the highest eCPM globally on both platforms; iOS generally pays more, especially in North America and Europe. — [Appodeal Mobile eCPM Report Q4 2024](https://appodeal.com/the-mobile-ecpm-report-updated-q4-2024-view); [PDF](https://appodeal.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Quarterly_Mobile_eCPM_Report_-_Q4_2024.pdf)
- 2026 vendor roundup: reported US rewarded-video eCPM ranges from about $13 to $30 depending on the source (more than a 2x spread); approximate Android rewarded video eCPM of about $13.6 in North America, $8.9 in Europe, $7.5 in APAC. Single-vendor, weakly sourced. — [Playio eCPM benchmarks 2026](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-ecpm-benchmarks-2026)
- Hybrid-casual: per Sensor Tower, hybrid-casual was the only segment to grow IAP revenue in 2025 (one summary: +20% to $4.2B) while traditional casual declined and other segments were flat. — [Deconstructor of Fun on State of Mobile 2026](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/2/state-of-mobile-2026); [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026)
- Premium paid mobile, Balatro (Playstack/LocalThunk, $9.99, launched on mobile late Sep 2024 after a Feb 2024 PC/console release): $500k in five days; about $1M in seven days; about $4.4M gross by late Nov 2024, all from the upfront purchase (AppMagic estimates, excludes Apple Arcade). — [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/balatro-mobile-hits-500k-in-five-days-tops-paid-game-charts/); [PocketGamer.biz, 7 days](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/balatro-approaches-1-million-in-seven-days-on-mobile); [PocketGamer.biz, $4.4M](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/balatro-nears-44m-on-mobile-amid-a-sudden-spending-surge)
- AppMagic 2025 data: Balatro earned an estimated $21.3M on mobile with more than 3.1M downloads in 2025; premium mobile releases were up 77% YoY to just under 750 paid titles in 2025; PC/console-to-mobile premium ports rose from 7 (2024) to 23 (2025). One port publisher says it stays selective (5-7 games a year) and leans on IP recognition, awards and proven communities; conclusion of the piece: mobile is a meaningful second SKU for the right premium game once PC/console has built awareness. — [Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/premium-mobile-games-increase)
- Apple's App Store lead (Carson Oliver) has said premium games are growing on the App Store and pitched flexible business models. — [iThinkDiff](https://www.ithinkdiff.com/apple-app-store-carson-oliver-indie-games-interview/)
- Vampire Survivors mobile (free, optional ads; released Dec 2022, so older than 2024): passed 3M downloads on iOS and Android just over a month after release; the developer rushed it out because of widespread cloning. — [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/vampire-survivors-mobile-hits-3m-downloads/)
- Direct-to-consumer web shops now represent more than 15% of total mobile gaming revenue (Newzoo estimate), reflecting large publishers moving payments off-store. — [PocketGamer.biz on Newzoo](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/global-games-revenue-surpasses-200bn-for-the-first-time-mobile-generates-113bn/)

### Inferences
- For an art-led solo developer there are effectively two workable models: (a) premium / "free to start with one-time unlock" on iOS-first as a second SKU after a PC launch, priced $5-10; (b) hybrid-casual F2P with rewarded ads + IAP, which requires live-ops, UA capital and usually a publisher. Model (a) fits "high visual quality" far better; model (b) fits "monetization at scale" but competes on marketability and data, not art.
- Ad monetization alone at Korea/US interstitial eCPMs of about $10-14 means roughly $0.01 per impression; a game needs hundreds of thousands of DAU to produce a living wage from ads only.
- The Vampire Survivors case shows an additional mobile-specific risk: a successful PC indie that delays its mobile version gets cloned on mobile first.

### Gaps
- No sourced ARPDAU figures specific to puzzle, hybrid-casual or idle genres for 2025-2026.
- No 2025-2026 benchmark data on subscriptions in games (battle-pass/VIP subscription conversion, share of revenue); only the store fee rates for subscriptions (section 5).
- No sourced mobile revenue for Dredge, Alto's Odyssey, Monument Valley 1-2 lifetime, or other premium-feel indie ports beyond Balatro; no mobile-vs-Steam revenue split for premium indies.
- Rewarded-video eCPM for Korea and Japan specifically (2025-2026) not found.

## 5. Store fees and policy as of late 2026 (Apple, Google, Epic cases, DMA, Korea/Japan)

### Takeaway
For a sub-$1M developer the effective store take is at most 15% on Apple (Small Business Program) and is falling toward 10% on Google Play as Google's post-Epic-settlement fee model rolls out by region (US/EEA/UK by mid-2026, Korea and Japan by end of 2026); external payment links are now allowed commission-free on iOS in the US (pending a Supreme Court review) and are being forced open in Korea, the EU and Japan. The bigger practical hurdles for a first-time solo developer are procedural: Google's 12-testers-for-14-days closed-test gate on new personal accounts and the 2026-2027 Android developer identity verification rollout.

### Cited Findings

- Apple Developer Program costs $99 per year; Google Play Console registration is a one-time $25 (long-standing official terms; official pages not re-fetched in this session). — [Apple Developer Program](https://developer.apple.com/programs/); [Google Play Console help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435)

## 6. Hypercasual / hybrid-casual / puzzle / idle genres and the role of publishers

### Takeaway
Pure hypercasual is a small (under $0.5B), paid-install-driven, low-margin niche; the growth is in hybrid-casual and puzzle (puzzle alone is about 44% of casual IAP revenue in H1 2026), which are publisher- and data-driven businesses where marketability metrics (CPI, D1/D7) decide greenlights, not art quality. Public information on current publisher deal terms is essentially absent.

### Cited Findings
- Hypercasual revenue remains under $0.5B versus about $22B for casual; its growth comes from new entrants rather than expansion (Azur Games analysis). Hypercasual was the only segment whose downloads did not decline in 2025 (Sensor Tower). — [PocketGamer.biz / Azur Games](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/what-happened-to-hypercasual-the-markets-evolution-over-the-past-year/); [Azur Games blog](https://azurgames.com/blog/what-happened-to-hypercasual-market-growth-over-the-past-year-and-where-it-stands-now/)
- Hybrid-casual: Sensor Tower has it as the only segment growing IAP revenue in 2025 (+20% to $4.2B in one summary). AppMagic (narrower definition; period may be Q1 2025 or 2025, unclear from the summary) reports hybrid-casual revenue of $788M (+75.1% YoY), downloads 14.1B (-4.1%), with puzzle the main driver (revenue +136.1%). The two trackers define the segment differently. — [GameDev Reports on Sensor Tower](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026); [AppMagic hybrid-casual report listing](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/hybdrid-casual-games-in-q1-2025/read)
- Puzzle: Sensor Tower Q1 2026 shows puzzle revenue up 21% YoY; AppMagic's H1 2026 casual report says puzzle generated $4.9B IAP in H1 2026 with over 44% share of casual, while overall casual revenue was flat YoY and growth concentrated in hybrid-casual and merge. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/puzzle-games-rise-as-mobile-genres-compete-for-attention-in-2026/); [AppMagic Casual Games Report H1 2026](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/casual-report-H12026)
- Hypercasual depends on paid installs for 81% (Android) / 67% (iOS) of traffic. — [AppsFlyer via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appsflyer-the-state-of-the-mobile)
- Prototype test thresholds: Supersonic states CPI is the most important KPI in prototype testing and suggests D1 retention of 38%+ on Android; a Voodoo publishing manager cited minimums of 55% D1 and 22% D7 (interview undated in results, likely hypercasual-era, pre-2024); a 2026 consultant blog gives D1 35-40% as "scalable", 45%+ "elite", D7 18-22% "scalable" (author's own experience, not industry-verified). — [Supersonic blog](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/the-3-most-important-kpis-for-testing-your-hyper-casual-prototype/); [PocketGamer.biz, "The Voodoo approach"](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/the-voodoo-approach/); [Game Growth Advisor, 2026-04-16](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-16-hybrid-casual-game-design-strategy-2026/)

### Inferences
- The publisher route (submit prototypes, get CPI/retention tests funded, publisher pays UA and takes a revenue/profit share) is the main way a no-capital small team can participate in F2P casual, but it selects for cheap-to-market mechanics and fast iteration (many prototypes killed), which is a poor match for a developer whose differentiator is polished art and animation in a single game.
- Publisher greenlight thresholds (D1 roughly 35-45%, D7 roughly 18-22%) are roughly at or above the GameAnalytics top-decile for all games (D1 about 40%), so most prototypes fail the test.

## 7. Does high art/animation quality pay off on mobile? Premium-feel indie hits, Apple Arcade, Netflix Games

### Takeaway
On mobile, visual craft pays off mainly through two channels that have both narrowed since 2024: platform-holder patronage (Apple Arcade, Netflix Games), which is shrinking and unreliable, and premium ports of already-famous PC/console indies, which is growing but depends on prior awareness. There is little evidence that art quality by itself drives revenue for an unknown mobile-first F2P game.

### Cited Findings
- Apple Arcade: developers told mobilegamer.biz (Feb 2024) of a "smell of death": falling upfront payments and shrinking per-play bonus-pool payouts since about late 2020, cancelled projects and opaque communication; a follow-up (Aug 2024) reported late payments (one indie unpaid for six months, a multi-month backlog on bonus-pool payments), stonewalled studios and poor support. A 2026 commentary piece says upfront funding and bonus payments have fallen further (weak source). — [mobilegamer.biz follow-up](https://mobilegamer.biz/inside-apple-arcade-again-late-payments-stonewalled-studios-terrible-tech-support-and-vision-pro-woes/); [MacRumors, 2024-02-26](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/02/26/developers-describe-apple-arcade-smell-of-death); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/apple-arcade-devs-allege-smell-of-death-amid-payout-reductions-and-canceled-projects); [remio.ai 2026 commentary](https://www.remio.ai/post/apple-gaming-strategy-expands-beyond-arcade-but-developers-still-need-proof)
- Apple still promotes indies editorially: its October 2026 Indie Games Week featured seven titles including Sifu and No Man's Sky (i.e. established PC/console ports), and App Store leadership says premium games are growing. — [remio.ai](https://www.remio.ai/post/apple-gaming-strategy-expands-beyond-arcade-but-developers-still-need-proof); [iThinkDiff](https://www.ithinkdiff.com/apple-app-store-carson-oliver-indie-games-interview/)
- Netflix Games: in mid-2025 Netflix removed about 20-21 licensed indie games (including Hades, Katana ZERO, Braid, Golden Idol and Monument Valley titles); under games president Alain Tascan the strategy narrowed to four categories (kids, party, narrative, mainstream/big IP) with a TV/cloud focus; reports say about a fifth of the mobile catalogue (120+ titles) was cut. In 2026 Netflix closed Night School Studio (Oxenfree) and Helsinki's Moonloot and cut internal game teams. — [9to5Mac, 2025-06-24](https://9to5mac.com/2025/06/24/netflix-is-pulling-the-plug-on-21-indie-games/); [AOL/Variety](https://www.aol.com/hades-monument-valley-more-indie-193508573.html); [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/netflix-to-close-la-studio-night-school-helsinkis-moonloot-and-cut-internal-game-teams/); [Engadget](https://engadget.com/2236683/netflix-shuts-down-oxenfree-studio-night-school-weeks-after-praising-its-really-solid-numbers/)
- Monument Valley 3 launched as a Netflix exclusive and later moved to a general mobile release (late 2025) as a free download with a one-time purchase for the full game; a third-party tracker estimates about 434K Google Play installs and over $100K monthly revenue on Android (unofficial estimates). — [PocketGamer.biz new release roundup](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/new-release-roundup-subway-surfers-monument-valley-3-hello-kitty-and-friends-world-and-more); [AppGoblin tracker](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.ustwogames.mv3)
- Balatro shows the premium-port ceiling: about $4.4M gross in two months (2024) and an estimated $21.3M and 3.1M+ downloads on mobile in 2025 at $9.99, after winning awards and selling millions on PC/console first. Premium releases rose 77% to just under 750 titles in 2025, and PC/console ports rose from 7 to 23. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/balatro-nears-44m-on-mobile-amid-a-sudden-spending-surge); [Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/premium-mobile-games-increase)
- Port publishers select on IP recognition, awards and proven communities. — [Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/premium-mobile-games-increase)

### Inferences
- The premium-feel mobile successes cited in the brief are either (a) ports whose audience was built on PC/console (Balatro, Vampire Survivors, Dredge, Sifu) or (b) products of platform funding that has since contracted (Monument Valley 3 via Netflix; many Apple Arcade titles). Neither path is open to an unknown first-time developer launching mobile-first.
- Notably, Balatro and Vampire Survivors are not high-art productions; their mobile success derives from design and fame. I found no evidence in this research that art/animation quality is a primary revenue driver on mobile; it mainly helps with editorial featuring (Apple) and platform deals, both scarce.
- Strategic implication: if visual quality is the developer's edge, mobile is better treated as a second SKU (iOS-first premium port) after proving the game on PC/console, with Apple featuring as upside.

## 8. Technical and operational burdens specific to mobile

### Takeaway
Beyond development, mobile imposes recurring compliance work (annual target-API bumps, identity verification, tester gates, privacy/attribution frameworks) and, for F2P, an expectation of continuous live-ops; most launched games peak in year one and die within three if not operated. These burdens are manageable for a premium offline game and heavy for an ad/IAP-driven F2P game.

### Cited Findings
- Google Play target-API treadmill: from 2026-08-31, new apps and every update must target Android 16 (API 36); already-published apps must target at least API 35 to stay visible to new users on newer Android versions; the bump recurs annually (API 37 next, no date yet). — [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk); [AppsOnAir explainer](https://www.appsonair.com/blogs/android-16-api-level-36-target-sdk-requirement-what-you-need-to-do-before-august-31)
- New personal Google Play accounts must complete a 12-tester, 14-day closed test per new app before production release. — [Choicely](https://www.choicely.com/blog/google-play-12-tester-rule); [Testers Community](https://www.testerscommunity.com/blog/google-play-closed-testing-requirements-2026)
- Android developer identity verification is rolling out to all developers (started March 2026; enforcement from 2026-09-30 in four countries, global in 2027), including for apps distributed outside Play. — [Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/android-developer-verification-rolling-out-to-all-developers.html?hl=mk); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-developer-verification-rollout-sideloading-flow-3653395)
- Google Play has been actively purging low-quality/non-compliant apps (total app count down about 47% from early 2024 to early 2025). — [Sensor Tower State of Gaming 2026 via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026)
- Privacy/attribution stack: iOS requires the ATT prompt for tracking (gaming opt-in about 38-39%), with SKAdNetwork/AdAttributionKit for aggregated attribution; on Android the advertising ID persists after Google ended Privacy Sandbox in Oct 2025. F2P games with ads therefore need an MMP/attribution SDK, ad mediation SDKs and consent flows. — [Adjust](https://www.adjust.com/blog/att-opt-in-rates-2025); [Aarki](https://www.aarki.com/insights/ios-attribution-in-2026-5-things-marketers-need-to-know/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-kills-privacy-sandbox-3608497)
- Live-ops expectation: Newzoo attributes mobile revenue growth to deeper monetisation of established live-service titles; SuperScale's survey (2023, older) found 76% of games peak in revenue within the first year and 83% die within three years; AppsFlyer and Adjust both describe 2025 as a shift of studio focus from acquisition to retention. — [PocketGamer.biz on Newzoo](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/global-games-revenue-surpasses-200bn-for-the-first-time-mobile-generates-113bn/); [TechSpot on SuperScale](https://www.techspot.com/news/100948-new-report-reveals-83-mobile-games-fail-first.html); [PocketGamer.biz on retention shift](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mobile-accounts-for-55-of-total-games-revenue-in-2025-as-studios-shift-towards-retention)
- Cloning risk: Vampire Survivors' developer rushed its mobile version because clones had already appeared on the stores. — [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/vampire-survivors-mobile-hits-3m-downloads/)
- Korea-specific regulatory attention on app review procedures: the Oct 2026 KMCC order also requires Apple and Google to improve their app review procedures. — [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/companies/20261028/korea-fines-google-apple-combined-476-mil-over-in-app-payment-practices)

### Inferences
- A premium, offline, no-ads game minimizes the SDK/privacy/live-ops burden (no ad mediation, no MMP, no consent management beyond basics) but still carries the annual OS-target updates on both stores, or the game is eventually delisted or hidden; a solo developer should budget maintenance time every year per shipped mobile title.
- F2P with ads/IAP roughly multiplies the operational surface: ad mediation, attribution, analytics, consent (GDPR/ATT), remote config, events/content cadence, customer support for purchases, and fraud/refund handling.

These are known burdens from general background knowledge but must be confirmed against Apple and Google developer documentation before being stated as fact.
