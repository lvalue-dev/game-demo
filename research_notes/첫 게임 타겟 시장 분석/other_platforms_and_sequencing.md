## 1. Per-platform access, revenue share, and indie opportunity (PlayStation, Xbox, itch.io, GOG, Discord, Telegram; Switch, Epic, web portals, UGC and subscriptions are detailed in their own sections)

### Takeaway
Console storefronts take roughly the same ~30% as Steam and are gated by approval and dev-kit programs (Xbox is the most open: free application, two free dev kits); itch.io and GOG are easy or curated side channels that add only single-digit percentages of revenue on top of Steam. Developer intent in 2026 is overwhelmingly PC-first: about 80% target Windows PC versus about 40% for PS5/Switch 2 and about 20% for Xbox.

### Cited Findings

Coverage cautions it measures intent and opinion, not shipped support. — [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/80-percent-of-game-devs-plan-to-target-windows-pc-in-survey-with-only-40-percent-for-ps5-and-nintendo-switch-2-and-20-percent-for-xbox-its-lucky-the-next-xbox-is-a-pc-then-eh); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/PS5-and-Switch-2-dominate-GDC-survey-with-only-20-of-devs-prioritizing-Xbox-consoles.1215786.0.html)

### Inferences
- For a solo Korea-based developer, Xbox is the lowest-friction console to get approved on (free kits, no fees) but also the smallest commercial target (20% developer interest, see also the Game Pass freeze in section 6); PlayStation and Switch 2 have more demand but more gating.
- itch.io is best treated as a free playtest, demo, and community channel rather than a revenue source; GOG is a small incremental add if accepted by its curation.
- Discord Activities and Telegram mini-games have no public evidence of meaningful premium-indie revenue in 2025-2026; they fit social, casual or multiplayer party concepts, not an art-led original-IP premium game.

## 2. Nintendo Switch / Switch 2: eShop state, discoverability, console-to-Steam ratios, porting houses

### Takeaway
Switch remains the most indie-friendly console in principle, but in 2025-2026 the eShop is crowded, sales for typical indies are widely described as weak, and Switch 2 dev kits were rationed (reports of only about 5% of indies, or one-third of prior Switch developers, having kits months after the June 2025 launch). Evidence on ratios points to Steam being the majority of revenue even with simultaneous launch, and late Switch ports adding on the order of 10% of Steam sales, with large outliers (Stardew Valley's Switch units are about 30% of its PC units).

### Inferences
- Stardew Valley's split works out to PC 63% / Switch 19% of lifetime units (Switch units = about 30% of PC units), which sits between the "10% of Steam for a late port" and "60-65% Steam with simultaneous launch" heuristics. A planning assumption of Switch = 10-30% of Steam revenue for a well-fitting indie genre, and less for a late or poorly-fitting port, is consistent with the evidence found, but there is no robust 2025-2026 dataset.
- For a first game, Switch 2 native access cannot be assumed; the practical path in 2025-2026 is a Switch 1 build (playable on Switch 2 via backward compatibility) or going through a publisher or porting partner who already holds kits and Nintendo relationships.
- The revenue-ranked eShop charts favour higher-priced, higher-quality games over $1 discount tactics, which marginally helps a premium art-led title, but eShop discovery still depends heavily on Nintendo featuring (Indie World, Directs) rather than organic browsing.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 eShop sales figures for small or median indie titles were found; Nintendo publishes none and developers rarely disclose.
- The GameDiscoverCo console/Steam ratio statements could not be opened to confirm date and exact wording; the most-cited issues are from 2020-2021.
- Switch 2 dev-kit price and whether access had normalised by late 2026 are unknown.
- Reliable porting-house pricing (typical fixed fees or revenue-share percentages) was not found; only vendor marketing ranges.
- The date of the MCV "cannot sell units on Switch" article was not confirmed.

## 3. Epic Games Store: terms, programs, actual sales volume vs Steam, mobile store

### Takeaway
Epic has the most generous terms in PC distribution (0% on the first $1M per app per year since June 2025, then 88/12; 100% for six months under opt-in First Run exclusivity), but the audience spend is small: $400M on third-party PC games in 2025 versus an estimated $14.98B gross for third-party games on Steam. For a small indie, Epic is a low-cost add-on, not a substitute for Steam.

### Cited Findings
- Announced May 1, 2025, effective June 2025: developers pay 0% on the first $1,000,000 of revenue per app per year, then the standard 88%/12%. The threshold is per app, not per developer account. Epic also announced developer-run webshops hosted by the Epic Games Store for out-of-app purchases. — [GamingOnLinux](https://gamingonlinux.com/2025/05/epic-reduce-their-cut-to-0-for-the-first-1-million-in-revenue-for-devs-on-the-epic-games-store); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/following-its-huge-win-over-apple-epic-says-it-will-take-even-less-money-from-developers-who-use-its-store); [MMO Fallout](https://mmofallout.com/2025/05/01/egs-drops-revenue-share-for-first-million)
- Comparison: Steam takes 30% up to $10M, 25% from $10M to $50M, 20% above $50M. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/following-its-huge-win-over-apple-epic-says-it-will-take-even-less-money-from-developers-who-use-its-store)
- Epic First Run: opt-in PC exclusivity giving third-party developers 100% of net revenue on eligible products for their first six months on the store; afterwards terms revert to 100%/0% up to $1M net revenue per product per calendar year and 88%/12% after. Exclusivity is PC-store only; developers can still sell via their own site and via Epic keyless redemption on stores such as Green Man Gaming and Humble Store. Eligible: new releases launching on or after October 16, 2023, without a pre-existing Epic exclusivity deal. — [Epic First Run program page](https://store.epicgames.com/features/epic-first-run-program); [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/epic-first-run-offers-developers-six-month-100-revenue-share-for-epic-games-store-exclusivity/1100-6517130/)
- Conflict: one source says eligible products must release on the Epic Games Store before June 30, 2025, while Epic's later-dated FAQ references the post-June-2025 0%/$1M terms. Current program status should be confirmed on Epic's page. — [Unreal Source](https://unrealsource.com/d/epic-games-store-incentive-programs-launch); [Epic First Run program page](https://store.epicgames.com/features/epic-first-run-program)
- Epic Games Store 2025 Year in Review (published early 2026): player spending on third-party PC games grew 57% to a record $400M (2024 baseline reported as $255M by one outlet, $250M by another); total PC player spending was $1.16B (+6%; one outlet says +5%); 78 million monthly active PC users in December 2025; 317M+ PC users; 6,000+ games. — [Epic Games Store](https://store.epicgames.com/news/epic-games-store-2025-year-in-review); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/epic-games-store-third-party-spending-rises-57-in-2025-interview/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/the-epic-games-store-had-a-record-breaking-2025-with-gamers-throwing-usd1-16-billion-at-the-6-000-plus-games-it-now-offers); [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/epic-games-store-saw-record-setting-third-party-sales-growth-in-2025-reaches-317m-pc-users/amp/)
- Same report: total gameplay hours on the store fell 14% year over year to 6.65B, while hours in third-party titles rose 4% to 2.78B. — [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/epic-games-store-2025-year-in-review); [inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/epic-games-store-reports-strong-sales-growth-even-as-player-hours-decline)
- Steam comparison (GameDiscoverCo estimate): $16.9B total gross revenue on Steam in 2025, of which $14.98B for third-party games. — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/revealed-the-numbers-behind-steams)
- Epic mobile store in 2025: began supporting third-party titles, launched its Free Games program on mobile, and improved store speed. No mobile spending or user figures were published in the coverage found. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/epic-games-store-third-party-spending-rises-57-in-2025-interview/); [Epic Games Store](https://store.epicgames.com/news/epic-games-store-2025-year-in-review)
- Paid exclusivity deals have shrunk: at GDC 2024 the developers of Slay the Spire and Darkest Dungeon said the Game Pass/Epic "gold rush is over" and that deal scale is significantly diminished. — [Pure Xbox](https://purexbox.com/news/2024/03/xbox-game-pass-indie-deals-have-come-down-in-scope-claim-devs); [KitGuru](https://www.kitguru.net/tech-news/mustafa-mahmoud/indie-devs-are-no-longer-feeling-the-support-from-game-pass-epic/)

### Inferences
- Epic's third-party PC spend ($400M) is about 2.7% of Steam's estimated third-party gross ($14.98B) in 2025. A small indie should budget for Epic revenue in the low single-digit percent of Steam revenue unless featured or included in the free-games program.
- Because the 0% tier is per app per year, virtually every first-time indie pays Epic nothing; the real cost of adding Epic is integration, QA and support time, not fees.
- First Run (six months of PC exclusivity for 100% revenue) is a poor trade for an unknown first title: it gives up roughly 97% of the PC audience during the launch window to save a fee that is already 0% on the first $1M.

### Gaps
- No developer-disclosed per-title Epic vs Steam sales comparison for 2025-2026 was found.
- Epic mobile store: country availability, iOS status outside the EU, number of third-party games, and install or spending numbers were not found.
- Whether Epic still pays minimum guarantees for exclusives or free-game giveaways to small indies in 2026, and at what size, is unknown.

## 4. Web portals (Poki, CrazyGames, Playgama, YouTube Playables)

### Takeaway
Web portals are a real, ad-funded business with very large audiences (Poki: 625M players in 2025; CrazyGames: 50M+ monthly), but the typical developer outcome is hundreds to low thousands of dollars per month per game, with only top developers reaching $1M per year. Splits are not transparently published. The format rewards instantly readable casual games, so it is a possible prototype-testing ground but a poor fit as the primary home of a premium art-led original IP.

### Cited Findings
- Poki welcomed 625 million players across desktop and mobile in 2025 and has a monthly audience above 100 million; it has not raised external funding. — [DigitalMediaNet (Poki announcement)](https://digitalmedianet.com/poki-announces-milestone-of-625-million-players-without-raising-external-funding/amp/); [Cinevva Poki guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/publish-game-poki)
- December 2025 Poki press release: revenues for its top developers increased tenfold in five years, "varying from $50,0000 to $1 million annually" (the lower bound is a typo in the source; unverified). — (source not pinned; via) [Webull/PR Newswire](https://www.webull.com/news/13970629037114368)
- Poki does not publish a universal revenue share; it negotiates terms per title through its developer portal. ### Inferences
- At $200-$3,000 per month, a portal web game can return a few thousand to a few tens of thousands of dollars per year; that suits small, quickly made casual games. A high-art, animation-heavy game has high per-unit production cost and large asset size, both of which work against the instant-load, ad-monetized format.
- As a testing ground, portals give fast, large-sample retention data for a core mechanic, but the audience (casual, younger, free-to-play, ad-tolerant) is different from Steam premium buyers, so validation transfers poorly to a premium PC launch. itch.io or a Steam demo is the closer proxy.

- Playgama's own terms and typical earnings were not found beyond its marketing claims.

## 7. Multi-platform sequencing: PC first vs mobile first vs simultaneous; port cost and timing; case studies

### Takeaway
The evidence and expert advice converge on PC (Steam) first, then consoles, then premium mobile once the game has proven demand: the indie hits that made significant mobile or Switch money (Stardew Valley, Balatro, Vampire Survivors, Slay the Spire, Dead Cells, Dave the Diver) all established themselves on PC first or simultaneously with PC, and port specialists explicitly say a game should already have commercial success and a community before porting to mobile.

### Cited Findings

PC launch was 2016, Switch 2017 (**[pre-2024]** launch dates). — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/cozy-farming-sim-stardew-valley-has-sold-over-41-million-copies-as-of-right-now-with-over-half-on-pc-and-almost-8-million-on-the-switch)

- Port timing and cost (blog/vendor estimates): 3-6 months per console platform plus 6-10 weeks certification; engine choice (Unity, Unreal, or engines with native console export) is the largest factor; planning for console constraints (memory, input, performance) from the start cuts cost; ports carry ongoing maintenance cost after launch. — [GameDo](https://gamedo-live.pages.dev/news/how-to-port-indie-game-consoles-2026); [Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-get-your-game-on-nintendo-switch); [Unity porting guide (PDF)](https://images.response.unity3d.com/Web/Unity/%7B2472c6b0-ed28-46c3-bd7a-12eed8ce3805%7D_Five-mistakes-to-avoid-when-porting-your-game-to-switch.pdf)
- Some indie ports to legacy consoles have been cancelled because projected sales did not justify the cost (single anecdote). — [allmind.ai news](https://allmind.ai/news/59f0438941c78c9ad94886860bb0e97d67d980fc2f9a5037ee69bd3c87fe1c1b) (low-quality aggregator)

### Inferences
- Pattern across cases: (a) Steam first, console 1-2 years later (Stardew, Vampire Survivors, Slay the Spire, Dave the Diver); (b) Steam and consoles simultaneously only with a publisher handling ports (Balatro with Playstack), then mobile about 7 months later. No case found of a premium indie succeeding mobile-first and then on Steam.
- Mobile ports of proven hits can earn seven to eight figures (Balatro about $9-21M, Slay the Spire about $12-14M, Dead Cells $6.5M), but these are the top of a category with only 23 ports in all of 2025; for a game without a PC hit, a premium mobile launch has little organic demand in a market where 96% of downloads are free-to-play.
- For a solo first-time developer the lowest-risk order is: Steam demo and launch → Switch (Switch 1 build, via publisher or porting house if Steam results justify it) → PlayStation/Xbox → premium mobile through a specialist (Playdigious-type) partner. Each later step is funded by, and only justified by, results from the earlier one.
- Designing for portability from day one (controller-first input, resolution-independent UI, modest memory budget, a mainstream engine) keeps port cost at the low end of the 3-6 month range.

### Gaps
- No explicit, recent (2025-2026) expert piece comparing PC-first vs mobile-first vs simultaneous launch was retrievable; Chris Zukowski's and GameDiscoverCo's console-timing posts could not be opened.
- No real port budgets from named porting houses; no publisher-disclosed port ROI.
- Per-platform splits for Balatro, Vampire Survivors and Dave the Diver are not public.
- Slay the Spire's unit sales by platform and Vampire Survivors' Switch sales were not found.

## 8. Indie publishers in 2025-2026: funding climate, deal terms, expectations of a first-time developer

### Takeaway
Publisher money is scarcer and smaller: industry investment fell from $12B (2021) to $2.4B (2024), Devolver's average spend per third-party title is falling from $2.99M (2022) to $1.04M (2026), and Humble Games collapsed. Typical deals in the Voyer Law dataset are a median advance of about $270k-$300k, a 50/50 split after recoup for funded deals (about 70/30 in the developer's favour without an advance), with the developer keeping IP in over 90% of cases. Publishers expect a polished playable build and evidence of traction rather than a concept.

### Cited Findings
- Industry investment fell from $12B in 2021 to $2.4B in 2024 (Game Developer item, May 2026). — [GameDev.net news mirror](https://gamedev.net/news/3916-were-going-to-see-more-hit-games-coming-from-publishers-youve-never-heard-of/) (source not pinned)

### Inferences
- With a median advance near $270k-$300k and most advances recoupable (a third at 100% publisher take until recouped), a small first title may see little post-launch income from a funded deal unless it sells well beyond the advance. A no-advance or services-only deal (about 70% to the developer) is the better structure if the developer can self-fund to launch.
- In 2025-2026 the realistic value of a publisher for a first-time solo developer is less the cash and more console access (existing Nintendo/Sony relationships and dev kits), porting, localization, QA, and platform featuring. That makes signing after a strong Steam demo or wishlist showing, with console/mobile rights as the negotiated asset, more favourable than signing early on a concept.
- Developer-run funds (Outersloth-type 50% then 15%) offer notably better post-recoup terms than the 50/50 median, but are highly selective.

### Gaps
